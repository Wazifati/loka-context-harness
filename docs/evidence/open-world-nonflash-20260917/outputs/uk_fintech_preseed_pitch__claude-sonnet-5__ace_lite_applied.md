# Applied Context

- **Objective:** Build a credible, honest pre-seed pitch package for Northline to raise £750k from UK/European early-stage investors within a 30-day prep window.
- **Audience:** Pre-seed VCs and angels investing in UK B2B fintech infrastructure/SaaS, comfortable with pre-PMF, pre-revenue-scale bets on team and wedge.
- **Approach:** Ground the narrative strictly in verified facts (2 paid pilots, 6 design partners, 41 interviews, prototype), separate evidence from aspiration, and build a 30-day plan that generates *more real evidence* rather than more polish.
- **Constraints:** £3,000 budget, 30 days, two founders, no audited data, no FCA authorisation, no lending/credit/advice claims, no fabricated market sizing or competitor claims, no return promises.
- **Deliverable:** Narrative framework, investor targeting profile, 10-slide outline, 5-minute talk track, 30-day evidence/outreach plan, objection list with honest answers, and a diligence checklist.
- **Success check:** Every claim in the deck/talk track must be traceable to something Northline can actually show or state as an assumption; nothing here should require you to say something untrue or unverifiable in the room.

## 1. Investment Narrative

**Core story arc:** *"UK micro-businesses fail or stumble not because they lack money, but because they can't see cash-flow trouble coming. Northline is the first product that stitches together invoices, bank feeds, and accounting data to show that gap early — without touching money, lending, or giving advice. We're B2B infrastructure, not a regulated financial product, which means faster time-to-revenue and a cleaner risk profile for early investors."*

Key narrative pillars (only claim what's true):
1. **Problem is real and personally validated** — 41 interviews, not a market report. Say "our interviews suggest," not "the market shows."
2. **Early signal, not proof** — 2 paying pilots at £350/mo + 6 unpaid design partners = people willing to engage repeatedly, not proof of scalable demand.
3. **Deliberately narrow regulatory footprint** — no client money, no lending, no advice = lower regulatory capital/authorisation burden, faster path to revenue. This is a *design choice*, worth stating explicitly as a risk-reduction feature.
4. **Founding team fit** — one technical, one commercial; be honest about the gap (no in-house compliance/security hire yet — plan to use the raise to close this).
5. **Use of funds is precise and time-boxed** — 18 months to prove retained usage, expand pilots into paid cohorts, and harden security posture before any regulatory-adjacent expansion (e.g., open banking scope).

**What NOT to claim:** total addressable market size, competitor comparison stats, projected ARR, "proven product-market fit," or any implication of FCA registration/approval unless it exists. If asked for TAM, say you have not built a verified market-sizing model yet and describe your bottom-up approach instead (e.g., "X million UK micro-businesses per ONS/Companies House category — we haven't yet validated addressable share and won't quote a number we can't defend").

## 2. Investor Profile to Target

Look for investors who:
- Invest at pre-seed/seed in **UK B2B SaaS or fintech infrastructure** (not consumer lending or payments-heavy funds — wrong thesis fit).
- Have a track record backing **pre-revenue-scale, pre-PMF** teams (2 pilots + prototype is normal for this stage — target funds whose portfolio shows this pattern).
- Understand **regulatory-adjacent-but-not-regulated** fintech (data aggregation, financial insight tools) — avoid funds only comfortable with licensed lenders/EMIs, since your positioning will look "too early" or "off-thesis" to them.
- Are UK-based or have strong UK/EU cross-border fintech pattern recognition (given FCA/GDPR/PSD2 open banking context even though you're not authorised yourself).

**Practical filter:** shortlist via public portfolio pages — look for 3–5 other portfolio companies doing bank-feed/accounting-integration or SME financial tooling. Angels with operational experience in accounting software, invoicing tools, or SME banking are high-value even at small check sizes.

*Validation needed:* You'll need to research actual funds/angels active in this space right now — don't rely on names I might guess, as fund theses and activity change fast and I can't verify current investment status.

## 3. Ten-Slide Deck Outline

1. **Title / One-line positioning** — "Northline: cash-flow visibility for UK micro-businesses, without touching their money."
2. **Problem** — grounded in interview findings (cite: "in 41 interviews with micro-business owners...")
3. **Why now** — open banking data availability + accounting software API maturity (factual, not hype)
4. **Solution / Product** — what it does, what it explicitly does not do (no lending, no advice, no custody)
5. **Product demo / prototype screenshots**
6. **Evidence to date** — 2 paid pilots (£350/mo), 6 design partners, 41 interviews — presented as early signal, not proof
7. **Business model** — pricing logic, who pays, unit economics *assumptions* (label as assumptions)
8. **Market approach** — how you'll find and convert the next 50–100 customers (channel logic, not sizing claims)
9. **Team** — two founders, honest skills gap, hiring plan from the raise
10. **The ask** — £750k, 18-month runway, use-of-funds breakdown, milestones you'll hit before Series A/next round

## 4. Five-Minute Talk Track

- **0:00–0:45** — Problem, grounded in real interviews. "We talked to 41 micro-business owners. The pattern that came up again and again was..."
- **0:45–1:30** — What Northline does and doesn't do. Be explicit about the non-regulated boundary — it's a strength, say it plainly.
- **1:30–2:30** — Show the prototype (or describe it vividly if no live demo slot). Connect it back to the specific pain point from interviews.
- **2:30–3:15** — Evidence: two paying pilots, six design partners, what you've learned from each, what's still unproven (retention, scaled willingness-to-pay).
- **3:15–4:00** — The ask and use of funds: 18 months, specific milestones (e.g., "convert pilots to a paid cohort of X, harden security, validate pricing").
- **4:00–4:45** — Team and why you two, plus the hire this money enables.
- **4:45–5:00** — Close: what you want from this investor beyond money (intros, domain expertise, patience for pre-PMF stage).

Practice for time — 5 minutes disappears fast with a demo included.

## 5. 30-Day Evidence & Outreach Plan (£3,000 budget)

**Days 1–7 — Tighten evidence base**
- Convert 2–3 of the 6 design partners into paid pilots or clear letters of intent (even nominal fee) — real conversion signal beats more interviews.
- Extract and document specific quotes/patterns from the 41 interviews into a one-page "evidence memo" for investor follow-up.
- Draft use-of-funds and milestone plan with founder-only labor (no cost).

**Days 8–14 — Deck and narrative**
- Build and test the deck with 3–5 friendly advisors/mentors (free, via network) — spend budget only if needed on design (~£300–500 for a freelance deck polish, optional).
- Record a short (2 min) prototype demo video as backup for remote pitches (~£0–200 with free/cheap screen-recording tools).

**Days 15–21 — Targeted investor list building**
- Build a researched list of 30–50 realistic-fit investors (see profile above) using public sources (Crunchbase free tier, LinkedIn, portfolio pages). Budget: up to £200–400 if using a paid list tool; otherwise free via manual research.
- Warm-intro mapping: identify who in your networks (advisors, pilot customers, interview subjects) can make introductions.

**Days 22–28 — Outreach**
- Send personalised outreach (no mass blasts) referencing specific portfolio fit.
- Book calls/meetings; use remaining budget (~£500–1,000) for travel to in-person meetings in London if needed.

**Days 29–30 — Rehearse and refine**
- Run 2–3 mock pitches with honest, critical feedback; refine based on real objections raised.

**Budget summary (indicative, adjust to actuals):** deck polish £300–500, demo/video tools £0–200, list-building tools £0–400, travel £500–1,000, buffer £900–1,700. Validate actual costs before committing — these are planning estimates, not quotes.

## 6. Likely Hard Objections — Honest Answers

**"You have no proof of product-market fit."**
Honest answer: Correct — we have early signal (2 paid pilots, 6 design partners, 41 interviews) but not proof. This raise is explicitly to convert that signal into evidence over 18 months, with clear milestones you can hold us to.

**"Two customers at £350/month isn't a business."**
Honest answer: Agreed, it's not yet. It tells us people will pay something for this problem when solved well. The next milestone is proving that holds at 20–50 customers, which this raise funds.

**"What stops a bank or accounting software vendor from building this themselves?"**
Honest answer: We don't have a defensibility claim beyond execution speed and focus — we haven't validated a moat and won't pretend otherwise. Our answer is depth of integration and specificity to micro-business cash-flow patterns, which we're still proving out.

**"Are you regulated? What about FCA?"**
Honest answer: We deliberately don't hold client money, lend, or give credit/investment advice, which keeps us outside the highest-friction regulatory categories. We have not sought or received any FCA authorisation, and we should not imply otherwise. If future features move toward regulated activity, that will require separate legal/compliance work we haven't scoped yet.

**"Your market size claims are missing — why?"**
Honest answer: We chose not to present a number we can't defend. We can share our bottom-up logic and the segment we're targeting, and we're open to building this out further with investor input.

**"What's your retention/churn data?"**
Honest answer: We don't have enough tenure yet on paid pilots to report meaningful retention. This is one of the primary things the raise is designed to generate evidence on.

## 7. Diligence Checklist (prepare in advance)

- Cap table and equity split between founders (and any advisor/design-partner equity, if promised)
- Company incorporation documents, Companies House filings
- IP ownership confirmation (code, product, any assigned IP from cofounders/contractors)
- Data handling policy — how invoice/bank-feed/accounting data is stored, processed, and secured (even pre-scale, investors will ask)
- Any data processing agreements with pilot customers
- Summary of the 41 interviews (methodology, who, how selected) — investors will probe for selection bias
- Pilot contracts/terms with the two paying customers
- Design partner agreements (even informal ones — clarify expectations, any equity/access granted)
- Technical architecture overview from your technical cofounder (especially data security given financial data handling)
- Financial model with clearly labelled assumptions vs. actuals
- Use-of-funds breakdown tied to milestones
- Any prior fundraising, SAFE/convertible notes, or advisor agreements already in place

**Validation note:** Some of this (data protection posture, IP assignment, any regulatory boundary questions) may benefit from a short session with a startup lawyer before the raise — I can't confirm your specific legal exposure, and this is exactly the kind of thing worth £300–500 of your budget if you haven't already had it reviewed.
