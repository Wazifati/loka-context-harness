# Applied Context

- **Objective:** Build a credible, fundable pre-seed pitch for Northline that is honest about early traction and avoids unverifiable claims.
- **Audience:** Private pre-seed investors in London and Europe, likely angel syndicates, micro-VCs, and fintech-focused funds.
- **Approach:** Structure the pitch around a clear problem, a focused product, honest evidence, and a realistic 18-month plan. Provide investor targeting, deck outline, talk track, 30-day plan, objection handling, and diligence checklist.
- **Constraints:** No invented market data, competitors, traction, legal requirements, compliance status, or guaranteed returns. Respect £3,000 budget and 30-day timeline. UK regulatory context must be stated carefully and only where factually safe.
- **Deliverable:** A complete pitch preparation pack with seven sections: narrative, investor profile, deck outline, talk track, 30-day plan, objections with answers, and diligence checklist.
- **Success check:** The output is specific, actionable, realistic, evidence-aware, and fit for a pre-seed fintech audience without overclaiming.

# Northline Pre-Seed Pitch Preparation Pack

## 1. Investment Narrative

**The core story:**

UK micro-business owners — sole traders, freelancers, and businesses with fewer than 10 employees — often cannot see a cash-flow problem until it is already happening. They have invoices in one place, bank transactions in another, and accounting software that updates too slowly. The result is late supplier payments, missed tax deadlines, and unnecessary stress.

Northline brings those three data sources together to show a business owner what their cash position is likely to be in the coming weeks — not just what it is today.

**Why now:**

- Open banking and accounting software APIs make it technically possible to access bank-feed and accounting data with customer consent.
- Micro-business owners are increasingly comfortable using digital tools for financial visibility.
- The founders have already spoken to 41 potential customers and have two paying pilots, which suggests the problem is real and worth solving.

**Why this team:**

- One technical cofounder and one commercial founder cover the two essential early functions.
- Six design partners and two paid pilots show early willingness to engage, even before the product is fully built.
- The team is not claiming product-market fit. They are claiming a well-researched problem and a credible first step toward a solution.

**What the £750,000 buys:**

- 18 months of focused work on product, security, and go-to-market.
- Enough time to move from clickable prototype to a working product used by a small number of paying customers.
- Enough evidence to support a credible seed round or to make a clear stop/go decision.

**What Northline is not:**

- Not a lender.
- Not a credit-decision tool.
- Not an investment adviser.
- Not holding customer money.

This matters because it reduces the regulatory surface area compared with many fintech businesses. The pitch should state this plainly but avoid claiming any specific regulatory status or exemption without professional advice.

## 2. Investor Profile to Target

**Primary targets:**

- **UK-based angel syndicates** that invest in B2B SaaS and fintech at pre-seed stage.
- **European micro-VCs** with a fintech or SME focus and cheque sizes of £100,000–£500,000.
- **Operators-turned-angels** who have built or sold B2B software, accounting tools, or SME fintech products.

**Secondary targets:**

- **Sector-specific angel networks** focused on financial inclusion, SME productivity, or open banking.
- **Pre-seed funds** that accept a lead investor plus follow-on angels.

**Investor characteristics to prioritise:**

- Comfortable with pre-revenue or very early revenue businesses.
- Understand that pre-seed is about team, problem, and early signal — not proven retention.
- Familiar with open banking, accounting software, or SME cash-flow pain.
- Willing to engage over a 4–8 week process rather than expecting a finished product.

**Investors to avoid or deprioritise:**

- Funds that require regulatory authorisation or FCA approval before investing.
- Investors who expect audited retention or large revenue multiples at pre-seed.
- Generalist investors with no fintech or B2B SaaS exposure who may misprice the risk.

**Practical targeting approach:**

- Build a list of 40–60 named investors or syndicates.
- Prioritise 15–20 for direct outreach.
- Use warm introductions where possible; cold outreach should be short and specific.
- Do not spray the same deck to everyone. Tailor the first email to the investor’s known focus.

## 3. 10-Slide Deck Outline

**Slide 1 — Title**
- Northline
- One-line description: “Cash-flow visibility for UK micro-businesses.”
- Founder names and roles.
- Raise amount: £750,000 pre-seed.

**Slide 2 — Problem**
- Micro-business owners cannot easily see upcoming cash-flow gaps.
- Invoices, bank feeds, and accounting software live in separate places.
- Late payments, missed tax deadlines, and avoidable stress are the result.
- Use one or two anonymised quotes from the 41 customer interviews.

**Slide 3 — Solution**
- Northline brings invoices, bank-feed data, and accounting-software data into one view.
- Shows likely cash-flow gaps before they happen.
- No lending, no credit decisions, no investment advice, no holding customer money.
- Show a simple product mock-up or screenshot of the clickable prototype.

**Slide 4 — Why Now**
- Open banking and accounting APIs make consent-based data access practical.
- Micro-business owners are increasingly comfortable with digital financial tools.
- The founders have direct evidence of demand from interviews and paid pilots.

**Slide 5 — Product**
- What the product does today: clickable prototype.
- What the next 18 months will deliver: working product, security hardening, and a small paying customer base.
- Key data sources: invoices, bank feeds, accounting software.
- Explicitly state what the product does not do.

**Slide 6 — Traction and Evidence**
- 41 customer interviews.
- 6 unpaid design partners.
- 2 paid pilots at £350 per month.
- Clickable prototype.
- Be clear: no audited retention data, no proven product-market fit.
- Frame this as early signal, not proof.

**Slide 7 — Business Model**
- B2B software subscription.
- Current pilot pricing: £350 per month.
- Future pricing to be tested during the 18-month runway.
- No claim about lifetime value, churn, or market size without data.

**Slide 8 — Market and Customers**
- Target customer: UK micro-business owners, sole traders, and businesses with fewer than 10 employees.
- Describe the customer segment qualitatively, not with invented market size figures.
- If a credible public source is available, use it and cite it. Otherwise, say the market is large but unquantified in this deck.

**Slide 9 — 18-Month Plan and Use of Funds**
- Product: build the working product from the prototype.
- Security: data protection, secure handling of bank-feed and accounting data.
- Go-to-market: convert design partners and pilots into a small paying cohort.
- Rough allocation: product and engineering, security and compliance, go-to-market and founder salaries.
- Milestones: working product, first 10–20 paying customers, retention signal, seed-round readiness or stop/go decision.

**Slide 10 — Team and Ask**
- Technical cofounder: relevant skills and what they will build.
- Commercial founder: relevant skills and what they will sell.
- Ask: £750,000 for 18 months.
- What the money unlocks: a credible seed round or a clear decision not to continue.

## 4. Five-Minute Talk Track

**0:00–0:45 — Problem**
“UK micro-business owners often cannot see a cash-flow problem until it is already happening. Their invoices are in one system, their bank transactions in another, and their accounting software updates too slowly. We interviewed 41 business owners, and the same pain came up repeatedly: they do not know what their cash position will look like in three or four weeks.”

**0:45–1:30 — Solution**
“Northline brings invoices, bank-feed data, and accounting-software data into one place. We show a business owner what their cash position is likely to be in the coming weeks, so they can act before a gap becomes a crisis. We do not lend, we do not make credit decisions, and we do not hold customer money.”

**1:30–2:15 — Traction**
“We have a clickable prototype, six unpaid design partners, and two paying pilots at £350 per month. We have completed 41 customer interviews. We are not claiming product-market fit. We are claiming that the problem is real and that a small number of business owners are willing to pay to explore a solution.”

**2:15–3:00 — Why now and why us**
“Open banking and accounting APIs make this technically possible with customer consent. We have one technical cofounder and one commercial founder, which covers the two functions that matter most at this stage. We have spent time with customers before building, which is why we are raising now rather than after building the wrong thing.”

**3:00–4:00 — Plan and use of funds**
“We are raising £750,000 for 18 months. The money goes to product, security, and go-to-market. Our milestones are a working product, a small paying customer base, and enough retention signal to raise a seed round or make a clear stop decision. We are not promising a large revenue number. We are promising a disciplined 18-month test of the business.”

**4:00–5:00 — Ask and close**
“We are looking for a lead investor and a small syndicate. We have 30 days of preparation ahead of us, and we are using that time to strengthen the evidence base and run a focused outreach process. If this is interesting, I would like to share the deck and arrange a follow-up conversation.”

## 5. 30-Day Evidence and Outreach Plan

**Week 1 — Evidence and narrative hardening**

- Write down every factual claim in the deck and mark whether it is supported by interview notes, pilot agreements, or prototype screenshots.
- Remove or soften any claim that cannot be supported.
- Draft the investment narrative and talk track.
- Prepare a one-page data room index listing what evidence exists and what is still missing.
- Budget: £0–£200 for any document tools or design support.

**Week 2 — Customer evidence and pilot documentation**

- Ask the two paying pilots for written confirmation of what they use, what they pay, and what problem they hope Northline solves.
- Ask the six design partners for one or two sentences on why they joined.
- Anonymise and organise quotes from the 41 interviews.
- If possible, record a short video or written testimonial from one pilot.
- Budget: £0–£300 for any small incentives or thank-you gifts, if appropriate.

**Week 3 — Investor list and outreach preparation**

- Build a list of 40–60 named investors or syndicates.
- Prioritise 15–20 for direct outreach.
- Write a short cold email and a short warm-intro request template.
- Tailor the first paragraph to each investor’s known focus.
- Prepare a one-page teaser separate from the full deck.
- Budget: £0–£500 for any CRM or list-building tools, if needed.

**Week 4 — Outreach and feedback loop**

- Send the first 10–15 outreach messages.
- Track responses and objections in a simple spreadsheet.
- Run at least two practice pitch sessions with a trusted advisor or fellow founder.
- Revise the deck and talk track based on feedback.
- Prepare the diligence checklist and data room.
- Budget: £0–£500 for travel to one or two in-person meetings, if useful.

**Total estimated budget:** £0–£1,500, leaving headroom within the £3,000 limit.

## 6. Likely Hard Objections with Honest Answers

**Objection 1: “You have no audited retention data. How do I know customers will stay?”**

Honest answer: “You do not know, and we do not know. We have two paying pilots and six design partners. That is early signal, not proof. The purpose of this raise is to build a product that can generate retention data. If we cannot show retention within 18 months, we will not be able to raise a seed round, and we will say so clearly.”

**Objection 2: “Why should I invest before you have regulatory clarity?”**

Honest answer: “Northline does not lend, hold customer money, make credit decisions, or give investment advice. That reduces the regulatory surface area compared with many fintech businesses. However, we have not obtained formal legal or regulatory advice yet, and we will not claim a specific status. Part of the raise will be used to get professional advice and document our position properly.”

**Objection 3: “Two paying pilots at £350 per month is not traction.”**

Honest answer: “We agree. It is not traction in the sense of proven demand. It is evidence that a small number of business owners are willing to pay to explore the problem with us. We are raising pre-seed to test whether that willingness can become repeatable revenue.”

**Objection 4: “The market is crowded. What is different about Northline?”**

Honest answer: “We are not going to claim a unique position we cannot prove. Our focus is narrow: UK micro-business owners who need forward-looking cash-flow visibility across invoices, bank feeds, and accounting software. We have spent more time with this specific customer than with a broad SME market. Whether that focus is enough to win is exactly what the next 18 months will test.”

**Objection 5: “Why do you need £750,000? That seems high for a prototype.”**

Honest answer: “The money is not for the prototype. The prototype already exists. The money is for 18 months of product engineering, security work, and go-to-market. It also covers two founder salaries, which is realistic for a focused pre-seed team. If an investor believes the plan can be delivered for less, we are open to discussing the budget line by line.”

**Objection 6: “What happens if you cannot raise the full amount?”**

Honest answer: “We will consider a smaller round if it still allows us to reach a meaningful milestone. But we will not take money that only funds a few months of work with no clear decision point. The £750,000 figure is based on what we believe is needed for a credible 18-month test.”

**Objection 7: “You have no proven product-market fit. Why now?”**

Honest answer: “We are not claiming product-market fit. We are claiming that the problem is real, the data access is now technically possible, and we have enough early signal to justify a disciplined test. Pre-seed is the stage where that test is funded.”

## 7. Diligence Checklist

**Founders and team**

- CVs or short bios for both founders.
- Summary of relevant technical and commercial experience.
- Any prior startup, employment, or domain experience relevant to fintech, SaaS, or SME tools.
- Founder time commitment and any other roles.

**Customer evidence**

- Summary of the 41 customer interviews: date range, customer type, key themes.
- Anonymised quotes or notes.
- Written confirmation from the two paying pilots: what they pay, what they use, what problem they hope to solve.
- Written or recorded feedback from the six design partners.
- Any evidence of willingness to pay beyond the two pilots.

**Product**

- Clickable prototype access or screen recording.
- Product roadmap for the next 18 months.
- Technical architecture summary, especially data sources and security approach.
- Any third-party APIs or services used or planned.

**Business model and pricing**

- Current pilot pricing and terms.
- Planned pricing assumptions and how they will be tested.
- Any customer acquisition cost data, even if early and anecdotal.
- Any churn or retention signal, clearly labelled as early and unaudited.

**Regulatory and legal**

- Current understanding of the regulatory position, clearly labelled as not formal advice.
- Any professional advice received or planned.
- Data protection approach, especially for bank-feed and accounting data.
- Company structure, shareholding, and any existing agreements.

**Financials**

- Current cash position and burn rate.
- Use of funds breakdown for the £750,000.
- 18-month budget with monthly or quarterly milestones.
- Any existing revenue from the two pilots.
- Any outstanding liabilities or commitments.

**Market and competition**

- Description of the target customer segment.
- Any credible public sources on market size, if available and cited.
- Known competitors or adjacent tools, without inventing a competitive landscape.
- Why the founders believe the focus on micro-business cash-flow visibility is worth testing.

**Risk register**

- Top 5–10 risks and how the team plans to address them.
- Honest statement of what is unknown: retention, regulatory position, product-market fit, and market size.

## Assumptions and Validation Needs

**Reasonable assumptions made in this pack:**

- The two paid pilots are genuine paying customers, not discounted or unpaid trials.
- The 41 customer interviews are documented and can be summarised for investors.
- The founders have not yet obtained formal legal or regulatory advice.
- The £750,000 is intended to cover founder salaries, product, security, and go-to-market for 18 months.
- The founders are willing to be honest about the absence of retention data and product-market fit.

**Validation needs before finalising the pitch:**

- Confirm the exact nature of the two paid pilots: contract terms, payment history, and what they currently receive.
- Confirm whether any formal legal or regulatory advice has been received.
- Confirm the current cash position and monthly burn rate.
- Confirm whether any credible public market-size data is available and can be cited.
- Confirm the technical feasibility of accessing bank-feed and accounting-software data via APIs for the target customer segment.

**Final note:**

This pack is designed to be commercially sharp without overclaiming. The strongest position for Northline is not to pretend the business is further along than it is, but to show that the founders understand the problem, have early evidence, and have a disciplined plan to test the business over 18 months. That is what credible pre-seed investors are looking for.
