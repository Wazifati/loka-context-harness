# Applied Context
- **Objective:** Build a credible 90-day route toward €3,000/month through ethical, AI-assisted education and implementation offers.
- **Audience:** Independent EU-based designers, copywriters, and consultants who need consistent expert-led marketing.
- **Approach:** Start narrow, validate demand through conversations and paid pilots, then use repeatable content to grow an owned audience.
- **Constraints:** 10 hours/week, €700 total budget, bilingual English/Spanish, no ads, manipulative tactics, copied content, or exaggerated AI claims.
- **Deliverable:** Positioning, niche, offer ladder, 90-day execution plan, content system, metrics, review rhythm, and initial experiments.
- **Success check:** Progress is measured by qualified conversations, email growth, conversion evidence, customer outcomes, and revenue—not vanity engagement.

## Core commercial assumption

€3,000/month is realistic as a **revenue target to work toward**, but it is unlikely to arrive reliably in the first 90 days from a 1,800-follower LinkedIn account and 320-person email list without an existing warm buyer base.

Your first 90 days should aim to prove three things:

1. A narrow audience recognizes a costly problem you can solve.
2. At least a few people will pay for a live, guided outcome.
3. Premium implementation is valuable enough to fund the business before low-cost products have significant volume.

A sensible 90-day target is **€1,500–€4,000 total collected revenue**, with a validated offer system that can reach €3,000/month afterward.

# 1. Positioning

## Positioning statement

> I help independent consultants and service professionals turn their real client expertise into a consistent, credible content system—with responsible AI as an assistant, not a substitute—so they can earn trust and create opportunities without becoming full-time creators.

## Sharper version for your LinkedIn headline

**EN:**  
Helping independent consultants turn client expertise into credible LinkedIn content, newsletters, and simple offers—with responsible AI, not generic AI slop.

**ES:**  
Ayudo a consultores independientes a convertir su experiencia real en contenido creíble para LinkedIn, newsletters y ofertas simples, usando IA con criterio.

## What you are selling

You are not selling “AI content creation.”

You are selling:

- a way to extract useful insight from existing expertise,
- a repeatable publishing workflow,
- professional judgment around where AI helps and where it should not be trusted,
- clearer positioning and offers,
- consistency without impersonality.

That distinction matters. Your prospects have likely seen enough generic “write 30 posts in 10 minutes” promises already.

## Your core message

> Your expertise is the raw material. AI can help you organize, challenge, adapt, and repurpose it. It cannot replace your judgment, evidence, client context, or voice.

Use this repeatedly. It is both your ethical stance and your category differentiation.

# 2. First niche to focus on

## Focus first on independent B2B copywriters in Spain and Portugal

More specifically:

> Freelance B2B copywriters with 3–10 years of experience who already deliver good client work but market themselves inconsistently.

### Why this is the best first niche

- They already understand writing and messaging, so they can quickly see why generic AI output is commercially weak.
- They have a direct financial incentive to attract better-fit clients.
- Their work naturally produces teachable insights: messaging decisions, buyer objections, website mistakes, conversion lessons, research findings.
- They are likely to buy templates, group programs, and implementation help.
- You can speak their language without needing to teach basic content principles first.

Designers and consultants can become adjacent segments later. Do not try to build separate messaging for all three at launch.

## First-niche promise

> Build a responsible AI-assisted authority system that turns your copywriting expertise into one credible LinkedIn post, one email, and one short video per week—without publishing recycled advice or spending your week online.

## Qualification criteria for early buyers

Prioritize prospects who:

- sell B2B services;
- charge at least roughly €1,000 per project or retainer;
- already have client experience and opinions;
- have a LinkedIn profile but post inconsistently;
- want more inbound opportunities, referrals, or authority;
- can invest €49–€500 now, or €1,200+ for hands-on help.

Avoid initially targeting beginners, job seekers, or people seeking “passive income with AI.” They will pull the offer toward low-value promises and intensive support.

# 3. Offer ladder

Build one system with three levels. Each level should solve the same problem at increasing depth.

| Level | Offer | Price | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Entry | **Expertise-to-Content Kit** | €29 early / €39 standard | Build trust, capture buyers, validate language |
| Core | **Credible Content Sprint** live cohort | €249 pilot / €349 standard | Primary scalable offer and proof engine |
| Premium | **Authority System Implementation** | €1,200–€1,800 | Reach revenue goals with limited client volume |

## A. Low-cost product: Expertise-to-Content Kit

**Format:** Notion or Google Docs toolkit, templates, examples, and a short walkthrough video.

**Promise:**

> Turn one piece of real client expertise into a credible LinkedIn post, newsletter section, and 60-second video outline—without handing your thinking over to AI.

**Include:**

1. Expertise extraction worksheet  
   - client questions you hear repeatedly  
   - opinions you hold  
   - mistakes you see  
   - decisions you make differently  
   - anonymized lessons from client work

2. Responsible AI prompt set  
   - idea expansion  
   - objection mapping  
   - structure and editing  
   - repurposing  
   - “generic-output detection” checklist

3. One weekly content-planning board

4. Ten annotated content examples  
   Show the source insight, the AI-assisted draft, and the human edits that made it specific.

5. Content credibility checklist  
   - Is this based on lived expertise?  
   - Are claims supportable?  
   - Is client information anonymized?  
   - Would this sound true if AI were removed?  
   - Does it say something more useful than generic advice?

**Do not** overbuild this before pre-selling it. Create the essential version in 6–8 focused hours after receiving initial demand.

## B. Live cohort: Credible Content Sprint

**Pilot format:** 3 weeks, live, small group, maximum 10 people.  
**Pilot price:** €249.  
**Delivery:** One 90-minute workshop weekly, one optional 45-minute co-working/Q&A session, templates, and light asynchronous feedback.

**Outcome:**

> By the end of three weeks, participants leave with a clear authority angle, a four-week publishing plan, and four pieces of content rooted in real expertise.

### Curriculum

**Week 1 — Find the expertise worth publishing**  
- define audience and authority angle  
- extract content from client work ethically  
- identify useful contrarian or experience-based points of view  
- create the first content bank

**Week 2 — Use AI without losing your voice**  
- responsible workflow: input, questioning, drafting, editing, fact-checking  
- build a personal prompt library  
- create LinkedIn post and newsletter formats

**Week 3 — Turn consistency into commercial opportunity**  
- create a lightweight publishing routine  
- connect content to a clear service offer  
- create a non-pushy call to action  
- review and improve four finished pieces

### Pilot revenue target

- 6 seats × €249 = **€1,494**
- 8 seats × €249 = **€1,992**

This is a better early target than expecting template sales alone to fund the business.

## C. Premium offer: Authority System Implementation

Do not frame this as generic “content management.” Position it as a strategic setup and implementation service.

**Promise:**

> In four weeks, build and install your AI-assisted authority system: positioning, content pillars, reusable workflows, templates, and an initial month of expert-led content.

**Scope:**

- 90-minute strategy and expertise extraction session
- LinkedIn positioning review
- authority angle and three content pillars
- personalized responsible-AI workflow
- 20–30 content ideas from their real expertise
- four polished LinkedIn posts
- two newsletter outlines
- four short-video scripts or outlines
- a four-week publishing calendar
- recorded handover and team/process documentation

**Price:** Start at **€1,200** for the first two pilot clients. Raise to **€1,500–€1,800** once you have clear case studies and a defined process.

**Capacity:** Maximum two active clients at a time.

## A viable €3,000/month mix

Examples after validation:

- 1 implementation client at €1,500  
- 4 cohort seats at €349 = €1,396  
- 4 template sales at €39 = €156  
- **Total: €3,052**

Or:

- 2 implementation clients at €1,500  
- **Total: €3,000**

The second model is simpler, but the first is healthier long-term because the cohort creates proof, community, and a pipeline for implementation.

# 4. The 90-day plan

## Time allocation: 10 hours per week

Use this default allocation:

- **3 hours:** Publish and repurpose one core insight
- **2 hours:** Relationship building and sales conversations
- **2 hours:** Build/improve offers and delivery materials
- **1.5 hours:** Email, admin, and follow-up
- **1 hour:** Metrics and weekly review
- **0.5 hours:** Buffer

Do not spend early weeks building a sophisticated website, large course, or extensive automation.

## Days 1–30: Diagnose, narrow, pre-sell

### Main objective
Validate that independent B2B copywriters will pay to become more consistent and credible with AI-assisted content.

### Week 1: Establish the message and invite conversations

1. Update LinkedIn headline, About section, and banner around the new positioning.
2. Create a simple interest form:
   - “I’m researching a small live sprint for independent B2B copywriters.”
   - Ask role, country, current content challenge, desired outcome, and willingness to join a 20-minute research call.
3. Publish one direct positioning post:
   - “AI can help a freelancer publish more. It cannot tell them what is worth saying.”
4. Send a short email to your list:
   - explain the shift;
   - invite relevant people to reply with their biggest consistency problem;
   - offer 10 research calls.
5. Personally invite 30–40 relevant LinkedIn connections to a research conversation. Do not pitch immediately.

**Target:** Book 8–10 conversations.

### Week 2: Run research calls and identify paid language

Use a 20-minute conversation structure:

1. What service do you sell?
2. How do new clients currently find you?
3. What have you tried with content?
4. What makes publishing difficult or frustrating?
5. Where has AI helped? Where has it made things worse?
6. If this were solved in four weeks, what would change?
7. Would a small live implementation sprint at €249 be worth considering?

Document exact wording. Reuse the phrases prospects use in your landing page and content.

**Target:** Complete at least 6 calls; identify repeated pains and objections.

### Week 3: Pre-sell the pilot cohort

Build a minimal sales page using a Notion page, ConvertKit page, or simple checkout page. It needs only:

- who it is for;
- the specific outcome;
- dates and format;
- what is included;
- price;
- a short FAQ;
- ethical AI boundary statement;
- application or payment link.

Publish a launch post and send two emails:

- **Email 1:** The problem and cohort invitation.
- **Email 2:** What participants will actually build, with a clear deadline based on start date—not artificial scarcity.

Invite everyone who showed genuine interest personally.

**Target:** 4 paid pilot seats by the end of Week 3.  
If fewer than 3 people buy after direct invitations and follow-ups, do not assume the price is the only issue. Revisit audience, promised outcome, and urgency of the problem.

### Week 4: Finalize and prepare delivery

- Confirm 4–8 participants.
- Build only the materials needed for Week 1.
- Create a baseline questionnaire:
  - current posting frequency;
  - email list size;
  - confidence level;
  - current inbound leads;
  - desired commercial outcome.
- Prepare the initial version of the low-cost kit from cohort materials.
- Offer one or two premium implementation calls to prospects who want hands-on support but cannot attend the cohort.

**Month-one target:** €1,000–€2,000 in cohort pre-sales and/or one premium client.

---

## Days 31–60: Deliver proof and launch the template product

### Main objective
Create customer outcomes, testimonials, and a practical entry product.

### Weeks 5–7: Run the cohort

Delivery priorities:

- Make participants publish, not merely learn.
- Give feedback based on specificity, usefulness, evidence, and fit with their offers.
- Capture anonymized before/after examples.
- Ask each participant for a midpoint check-in and a final testimonial request.

During the cohort, publish content based on recurring lessons:

- “Three ways experienced freelancers accidentally make AI-written content sound interchangeable.”
- “The difference between a content prompt and an expertise-extraction prompt.”
- “How one client question can become a post, email, and video—without repeating yourself.”

### Week 8: Launch the Expertise-to-Content Kit

Launch it first to:

1. cohort waitlist;
2. email subscribers;
3. LinkedIn followers who engaged with your series;
4. people who attended research calls but did not join the cohort.

Use a modest introductory price: **€29 for seven days**, then €39. The lower price is not fake urgency if the date and future price are genuine.

**Target:** 15 sales × €29 = **€435**.  
Treat that as validation and list-building, not a major revenue engine yet.

### Upgrade path

Every template buyer should receive a simple next step:

> “If you want feedback, accountability, and a completed four-week system, join the next Credible Content Sprint.”

Every cohort participant should receive:

> “If you want this system installed around your exact offers, client examples, and workflow, apply for implementation.”

---

## Days 61–90: Repeat, raise confidence, sell implementation

### Main objective
Use proof from the pilot to fill the next cohort and close one premium implementation client.

### Weeks 9–10: Publish proof and open cohort two

Create content from real outcomes, with permission:

- before/after examples;
- lessons from participant workflows;
- objections they overcame;
- process insights;
- anonymized numbers only when meaningful and accurate.

Run Cohort 2 at **€299–€349** if pilot feedback and outcomes support the increase.

**Target:** 6 seats × €299 = **€1,794**.

### Weeks 11–12: Sell implementation selectively

Offer implementation to:

- cohort members who need personalization;
- experienced service providers who want execution done with them;
- people whose content is already partially working but lacks a system.

Do not pitch it broadly as “I write your posts.” Sell the commercial system: authority, workflow, content assets, and handover.

**Target:** One €1,200–€1,500 implementation sale.

### End-of-quarter review

At day 90, decide based on data:

- Keep serving copywriters only if they are converting.
- Add independent designers only if you have a separate, evidenced message.
- Keep the cohort if it delivers clear outcomes at a reasonable preparation-to-revenue ratio.
- Raise prices only after demand and delivery evidence, not because a pricing guide says to.

# 5. One repeatable content series

## Series name: “One Client Insight, Three Useful Assets”

**Purpose:** Demonstrate your method every week rather than merely describing it.

Each week, take one anonymized real-world observation from freelance service work and turn it into:

1. a LinkedIn post;
2. an email;
3. a short YouTube video.

The point is not maximum output. It is demonstrating how expert judgment becomes useful, multi-format content.

## Weekly production system

### Step 1: Capture one insight — 20 minutes

Choose one of these sources:

- a recurring client question;
- a mistake you keep seeing;
- a decision you make differently from less experienced peers;
- a framework you use;
- an ethical AI boundary you encountered;
- an objection to your offer.

### Step 2: Make the source explicit — 15 minutes

Write:

- What happened?
- What did it reveal?
- What is the useful lesson?
- Who needs to hear it?
- What evidence or experience supports it?
- What should not be generalized?

Then use AI to challenge structure, find gaps, or suggest angles—not to invent expertise.

### Step 3: Create the three assets — 90 minutes

| Channel | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 400–700-word story or lesson | “The AI content problem is often not the prompt. It is the missing opinion.” |
| Email | 250–450-word personal note + practical takeaway | “This week’s field note: the client question behind a better post.” |
| YouTube | 3–6-minute teaching video | “How to turn one client question into credible content with AI.” |

### Step 4: Use one consistent CTA — 10 minutes

For the first month, use one CTA only:

> Reply with “SYSTEM” if you want my checklist for turning client expertise into content without sounding generic.

This starts conversations and identifies warm leads. Send the checklist manually at first. Ask one follow-up question:

> Which part is hardest for you right now: finding ideas, writing in your voice, or publishing consistently?

That response creates research, relationships, and future sales conversations.

## Content ratio

Use:

- **60%** practical teaching and useful examples
- **25%** informed opinions and ethical boundaries
- **15%** offers, case studies, and invitations

You do not need to post daily. One strong LinkedIn post, one email, and one YouTube video weekly is enough for the first 90 days.

# 6. Metrics that matter

Track weekly in a simple spreadsheet or Notion dashboard.

## Audience and attention

- LinkedIn post impressions and meaningful comments
- LinkedIn profile views
- new relevant connection requests
- email subscriber growth
- email open rate and reply rate
- YouTube views and average watch time

These are directional indicators, not proof of business health.

## Demand and sales

More important metrics:

| Metric | 90-day working target |
|---|---:|
| Research conversations completed | 12–15 |
| Qualified sales conversations | 10 |
| Cohort seats sold | 10–14 total |
| Template sales | 15–30 |
| Premium implementation clients | 1–2 |
| Total revenue | €1,500–€4,000 |
| Testimonials/case studies | 3–5 |

## Conversion benchmarks to watch

These are not universal rules, but useful signals:

- **Research call to paid cohort:** aim for 15–30% once the offer is well matched.
- **Warm sales conversation to premium client:** aim for 10–20%.
- **Email click to template purchase:** early conversion may be low; focus on buyer feedback and list quality.
- **Content CTA replies:** 3–10 meaningful replies per month is more useful than hundreds of passive likes.

## Leading indicator of a good offer

The best signal is not “people say this sounds great.”

It is one of these:

- they pay;
- they ask when the next cohort is;
- they introduce a peer;
- they ask for more hands-on help;
- they use language that clearly connects the offer to a business problem.

# 7. Weekly review rhythm

Schedule a 45–60 minute review every Friday.

## Review these five questions

1. **What content generated qualified conversations, not just engagement?**
2. **What exact audience language did I hear this week?**
3. **Where did people hesitate before buying?**
4. **What delivery task took too long and should become a template?**
5. **What single change will I test next week?**

## Keep a simple scorecard

| Area | Weekly question |
|---|---|
| Publishing | Did I publish one core insight in all three formats? |
| Relationships | Did I initiate or follow up with 5–10 relevant people? |
| Sales | Did I make clear invitations to a paid next step? |
| Delivery | Did clients or participants complete a meaningful outcome? |
| Learning | Did I record objections, questions, and message language? |

## Decision rules

- If content gets attention but no conversations, improve the CTA and audience specificity.
- If conversations are warm but nobody buys, clarify the paid outcome or adjust the offer format.
- If the cohort sells but delivery overwhelms your 10 hours/week, reduce customization and strengthen templates.
- If premium prospects want “done for you,” maintain your boundary: implementation is collaborative and expertise-led, not automated content outsourcing.

# 8. First three experiments to run

## Experiment 1: Niche-message test

**Hypothesis:** Independent B2B copywriters respond more strongly to “credible content from real client expertise” than to broad AI-content education.

**Run it:**
- Publish two LinkedIn posts one week apart.
- Post A: written explicitly for B2B copywriters.
- Post B: written for freelancers generally.
- Use the same CTA: reply “SYSTEM” for the checklist.

**Measure:**
- qualified replies;
- profile views from relevant people;
- research-call bookings.

**Decision:** Use the message that produces more conversations with people who can buy—not simply more impressions.

---

## Experiment 2: Paid pilot pre-sale

**Hypothesis:** Your audience will pay €249 for a small, live cohort with feedback and completed content assets.

**Run it:**
- Invite research-call participants, engaged subscribers, and relevant connections.
- Offer 6–8 seats.
- Give a real enrollment deadline tied to cohort start date.
- Ask for payment or a meaningful deposit to reserve a place.

**Measure:**
- number of paid seats;
- objections;
- participant profile;
- time required to sell each seat.

**Decision:**  
- 4+ paid seats: run it.  
- 2–3 paid seats: run a smaller version only if delivery is still worthwhile and feedback is strategically valuable.  
- 0–1 paid seats: interview non-buyers, revise the promise, and do not build more material yet.

---

## Experiment 3: Template demand test

**Hypothesis:** A practical template kit is a credible low-friction entry point for people who are not ready for live support.

**Run it:**
- Create a lean kit with one strong worksheet, one content workflow, five prompts, and two examples.
- Offer it to your list and LinkedIn audience at €29.
- Include a question in the purchase flow: “What made you buy this now?”

**Measure:**
- purchases;
- conversion from warm leads;
- buyer responses;
- upgrades or requests for cohort support.

**Decision:** Keep and improve the product if buyers use it and it generates cohort leads. Do not spend weeks adding templates merely to increase perceived volume.

# 9. Budget allocation

Keep spending low until the offer is validated.

| Item | Budget | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Email/landing page/payment tools | €100–€180 | Use existing tools where possible |
| Video/audio basics or captions | €50–€100 | Only if current setup blocks publishing |
| Design/template assets | €50–€100 | Keep simple; your audience values utility |
| Cohort support/tools | €50–€100 | Zoom, workspace, scheduling, etc. |
| Customer research incentives or contingency | €100–€150 | Optional; do not pay for engagement |
| Reserve | €170–€350 | Keep for needs revealed by validation |

Do not spend the €700 on branding, a new website, expensive course platforms, or elaborate production equipment.
