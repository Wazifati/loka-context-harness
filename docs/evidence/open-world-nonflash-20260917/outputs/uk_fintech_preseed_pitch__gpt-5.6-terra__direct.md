## 1. Core investment narrative

### One-sentence pitch
**Northline is cash-flow intelligence software for UK micro-businesses: it brings together invoices, bank-feed data and accounting data to help owners see likely cash-flow gaps earlier and understand what is driving them.**

### The problem
Micro-business owners often manage cash reactively. Their information is fragmented across bank accounts, invoices, accounting software, spreadsheets and their own memory. The important question is not simply “what is my bank balance?” but:

- What cash is likely to arrive, and when?
- What payments and liabilities are likely to leave the account?
- Where could a gap emerge?
- What assumptions are causing that risk?
- What should I check or act on now?

Many small businesses already have accounting tools and bank data, but owners may not have a clear, timely operating view of near-term cash position.

### Northline’s wedge
Northline should not position itself as “AI that predicts the future” or as a lending product. It is better positioned as:

> **An explainable cash-flow visibility layer for micro-business owners.**

Its value is in combining existing operational data into a practical, understandable view:

- Invoices and expected payment timing
- Bank-feed transaction history and current cash position
- Accounting-system data
- A forward-looking cash view
- Clearly labelled assumptions, alerts and drivers of possible gaps

The product should help owners make better-informed operational decisions; it should not claim to guarantee outcomes.

### Why now
The “why now” argument should be evidence-led rather than based on unsupported market claims:

1. **Relevant data is increasingly digital.** Many micro-businesses use bank feeds, invoicing tools and accounting software, creating the possibility of a more joined-up cash view.
2. **Owners need operational clarity, not more dashboards.** The problem is not only access to data; it is translating fragmented data into a simple, actionable picture.
3. **The initial product can be narrow and useful.** Northline does not need to replace accounting software or become a bank. It can begin as a focused layer on top of systems customers already use.
4. **The founding team has early evidence of pain.** Northline has conducted 41 customer interviews, has six unpaid design partners, and has two paid pilots at £350 per month. That is not product-market fit, but it is enough to justify disciplined testing.

### The investment case
Investors are not being asked to believe that Northline has already solved a huge market. They are being asked to fund a focused de-risking plan:

> **Use £750,000 to turn early problem evidence and paid pilots into a secure, repeatable product and a credible go-to-market motion over 18 months.**

The capital should aim to prove four things:

1. **Product:** customers will regularly use Northline’s cash-flow view.
2. **Value:** Northline helps customers identify and understand potential cash pressure earlier.
3. **Commercial model:** the team can convert pilots and design partners into paying customers at sustainable pricing.
4. **Trust:** Northline can handle sensitive financial data with appropriate security, permissions, governance and legal advice.

### Claim discipline for the pitch
Use language such as:

- “helps owners understand likely cash-flow gaps”
- “combines data sources into an explainable forward-looking view”
- “is designed to complement, not replace, accounting software”
- “early pilots suggest willingness to pay”
- “we are testing whether this becomes a repeatable, valuable workflow”

Avoid language such as:

- “guarantees cash-flow forecasting accuracy”
- “prevents business failure”
- “makes credit decisions”
- “is FCA regulated” unless that is factually true and applicable
- “we have product-market fit”
- “we own the market”
- “AI-powered” unless you can explain exactly what it does and validate its performance

## 2. Investor profile to target

Northline is most likely to resonate with investors who understand early-stage B2B software, fintech infrastructure, accounting ecosystems or small-business workflows.

### Primary target investors
Target UK and European investors with a demonstrated interest in:

- Pre-seed or very early seed B2B SaaS
- Fintech software, rather than only regulated financial products
- Accounting, payments, banking, finance-operations or SME tooling
- Technical products involving integrations, data security and workflow adoption
- Businesses selling to small businesses, accountants, bookkeepers or financial advisers

### Ideal investor characteristics
Look for investors who can contribute more than capital:

| Characteristic | Why it matters for Northline |
|---|---|
| Experience in B2B SaaS | Helps with pricing, retention, onboarding and sales motion |
| Fintech/data experience | Helps with trust, data permissions, security and integration questions |
| SME or accounting ecosystem knowledge | Helps identify customer channels and partnership routes |
| Comfortable with pre-revenue / early revenue | Northline is still proving repeatability |
| Willing to fund an evidence plan | The round is about de-risking, not scaling an already-proven engine |
| Strong follow-on network | Important if Northline reaches a seed round after 12–18 months |

### Investor categories
Build a balanced target list across:

1. **UK pre-seed and seed funds**  
   Focus on B2B software and fintech investors willing to lead or co-lead a £750,000 round.

2. **European early-stage funds**  
   Particularly those investing cross-border in UK software businesses and with fintech or SME-software portfolios.

3. **Operator angels**  
   Useful profiles include former founders or senior leaders from:
   - accounting software companies;
   - SME banking;
   - payments businesses;
   - finance software;
   - bookkeeping and accounting firms;
   - B2B SaaS companies;
   - security and data infrastructure businesses.

4. **Strategic angels, carefully selected**  
   Accountants, bookkeepers, SME advisers and relevant software operators can be valuable, but avoid investors whose commercial interests could create unnecessary conflicts or restrict future partnerships.

### Who not to prioritise
Deprioritise investors who:

- Only invest at later-stage seed or Series A.
- Require material recurring revenue or audited retention before engaging.
- Primarily invest in consumer fintech, lending or regulated financial products.
- Expect Northline to have already solved regulatory, distribution and retention questions.
- Are unlikely to understand a software-led business that handles financial data without holding funds or making credit decisions.

### Practical fundraising structure
For a £750,000 round, seek:

- One credible lead or anchor investor, if possible.
- A small syndicate of sector-relevant funds and angels.
- Investors who can support the next raise, not merely fill the current round.

Do not optimise only for valuation. At this stage, investor quality, ability to help recruit, customer introductions, product judgement and future-financing credibility can matter more.

## 4. Five-minute talk track

This is approximately 650–750 words when delivered naturally.

---

**“Northline is building cash-flow intelligence software for UK micro-business owners.**

For many owners, cash management is still fragmented and reactive. They may have a bank account, invoices, accounting software and a spreadsheet, but they do not have one clear answer to a basic operating question: *where might cash become tight in the coming weeks, and why?*

A bank balance tells you what happened. An accounting system can contain valuable information. But for a busy owner, turning those inputs into an understandable near-term cash view can still be manual, time-consuming and uncertain.

Northline brings together invoice information, bank-feed data and accounting-software data to help owners see likely cash-flow gaps earlier and understand the factors driving them. The goal is not to make unrealistic guarantees about the future. It is to give the owner a clearer, explainable view so they can investigate, prioritise and act sooner.

Northline is B2B software. We do not hold customer funds, lend, make credit decisions or provide investment advice. We are focused on a narrower problem: helping a business owner understand their own operational cash position.

We are early, and we are deliberate about what we have and have not proven. So far, we have completed 41 customer interviews, have six unpaid design partners, two paid pilots at £350 per month, and a clickable prototype. These are encouraging signals that the problem is real and that some customers are willing to pay to explore a solution. They are not yet product-market fit, audited retention data or a scalable sales engine.

That is precisely what this round is for.

Our initial wedge is a micro-business that already has digital financial data but lacks a practical joined-up cash workflow. We will begin with founder-led sales and close customer collaboration. Our aim is to identify the customer profile with the strongest pain, build the smallest product that creates recurring value, and then test whether that value can be delivered and sold repeatedly.

The product experience will be simple: connect relevant data sources, establish a current cash position, incorporate invoice and accounting information, show a forward-looking cash view, and explain potential gaps and the assumptions behind them. Trust is central. Because we handle sensitive business financial data, security, permissions, privacy and data reliability are core product work, not a later add-on.

We are raising £750,000 for 18 months. The funds will support product and engineering, core integrations, security and privacy work, specialist legal and compliance advice where needed, and a disciplined go-to-market programme.

We are looking for investors who understand pre-seed B2B SaaS and fintech data products, and who can help us build a trusted product for a meaningful but underserved operational problem.

Northline is not claiming to have solved cash flow for every small business. We are building a focused software layer to make cash risk more visible and understandable—and we have early evidence that this is a problem worth solving.”**

## 5. Thirty-day evidence and outreach plan

The objective is not to manufacture traction in 30 days. It is to convert existing activity into investor-grade evidence, sharpen the story and begin disciplined outreach.

### Principles
- Do not optimise for vanity metrics.
- Do not describe conversations as customers.
- Do not call unpaid design partners “active users” unless they are genuinely using a product.
- Gather evidence investors can inspect: customer notes, pilot agreements, product screenshots, data-security decisions, pipeline records and financial model assumptions.
- Obtain permission before using names, logos, quotes or case studies.

### Week 1: Positioning, customer evidence and fundraising foundations
**Objectives**
- Set the narrative.
- Organise existing evidence.
- Identify the key hypotheses to test.

**Actions**
1. Write a one-page investment memo:
   - problem;
   - customer;
   - product;
   - current evidence;
   - what remains unknown;
   - raise and milestones.

2. Audit the 41 interview notes:
   - segment interviewees by business type, size, software used and cash-flow pain;
   - identify recurring language;
   - isolate strongest evidence of urgency, current workaround and willingness to pay;
   - record objections and reasons for non-interest.

3. Create an evidence table:
   - Interview count;
   - Design partners;
   - Paid pilots;
   - Pilot status;
   - Product access;
   - Feedback;
   - Price;
   - Decision-maker;
   - Next step.

4. Define 3–5 core hypotheses:
   - Which customer profile has the strongest recurring pain?
   - Which data sources are essential for initial value?
   - What is the minimum useful workflow?
   - What user action follows a “likely gap” alert?
   - What price and onboarding effort are viable?

5. Build deck version one and a short investor memo.

6. Assemble a target list of investors and warm-introduction paths.

**Outputs by end of week**
- Draft deck.
- One-page memo.
- Customer evidence summary.
- Investor CRM/list.
- Prioritised product hypotheses.

---

### Week 2: Turn pilots into evidence
**Objectives**
- Deepen learning with the two paid pilots.
- Seek conversion or expansion from design partners where appropriate.
- Improve product evidence.

**Actions**
1. Run structured weekly sessions with both paid pilots:
   - What did they use?
   - What did they expect?
   - What did they trust or not trust?
   - What did they do differently because of the information?
   - What value would justify continued payment?
   - What would make them cancel?

2. Ask for specific permission-based evidence:
   - anonymous quote;
   - case-study participation;
   - product feedback session;
   - referral to one relevant peer;
   - pilot extension or conversion discussion.

3. Re-engage all six design partners:
   - share what has changed;
   - ask for a commitment level: active feedback, pilot participation, paid conversion conversation, or pause;
   - avoid counting inactive partners as traction.

4. Conduct 8–12 additional interviews only if they test a defined hypothesis.  
   Better questions:
   - “Walk me through the last time cash was unexpectedly tight.”
   - “What information did you have, and what was missing?”
   - “What tools do you currently use?”
   - “Who owns this problem?”
   - “What would make you trust a forecast?”
   - “What would you pay for if it saved time or surfaced risk earlier?”

5. Produce a short product demo video, ideally 90–120 seconds.

**Outputs by end of week**
- Pilot learning report.
- Updated product screenshots or prototype.
- At least one permissioned customer quote if available.
- Defined early adopter profile.
- Refined deck.

---

### Week 3: Investor materials, diligence preparation and soft outreach
**Objectives**
- Be ready for investor scrutiny.
- Start conversations before formally launching the round.

**Actions**
1. Build a lightweight data room.
2. Create:
   - cap table;
   - 18-month operating model;
   - use-of-funds summary;
   - product roadmap;
   - security/privacy overview;
   - customer pipeline;
   - interview and pilot evidence summary;
   - founder biographies;
   - corporate document index.

3. Obtain appropriate professional advice on:
   - data protection roles and documentation;
   - customer terms;
   - data-access permissions;
   - whether any planned product features could affect regulatory perimeter;
   - marketing and claims review.

Do not present legal conclusions unless confirmed by qualified advisers.

4. Ask for warm introductions.  
   Send a short, clear message:
   - what Northline does;
   - early evidence;
   - amount being raised;
   - why the recipient is relevant;
   - request for a 20–30 minute conversation.

5. Begin soft-circling 10–20 high-fit investors and angels.  
   The purpose is feedback, relationship building and identifying likely lead interest—not claiming the round is already competitive.

**Outputs by end of week**
- Investor-ready deck.
- Data room v1.
- Financial model.
- Outreach message templates.
- Initial investor conversations booked.

---

### Week 4: Formal outreach and iteration
**Objectives**
- Run a focused process.
- Improve materials based on investor feedback.
- Build momentum through quality, not artificial urgency.

**Actions**
1. Start formal outreach to the full priority list.
2. Track every investor interaction:
   - date;
   - source of introduction;
   - fit;
   - stage;
   - questions;
   - next step;
   - follow-up date;
   - feedback themes.

3. Run founder practice sessions:
   - five-minute pitch;
   - 15-minute deck;
   - 30-minute diligence discussion;
   - hostile-question practice.

4. Update the deck only when feedback reveals a real gap:
   - unclear customer segment;
   - vague pricing;
   - unconvincing security approach;
   - unclear route to customers;
   - unsupported claims;
   - weak milestone definition.

5. Send concise updates to engaged investors:
   - pilot development;
   - product progress;
   - new customer evidence;
   - funding progress only if truthful and specific.

**Outputs by day 30**
- Active investor pipeline.
- Refined narrative and deck.
- Diligence-ready document set.
- Better-defined product and customer hypotheses.
- A clear list of the next 90-day evidence milestones.

## 7. Likely hard objections and honest responses

### Objection 1: “You only have two paying pilots. Why raise £750,000 now?”
**Honest answer:**  
“That is a fair challenge. We are not presenting two pilots as proof of a scalable business. We are raising to turn early problem validation into product, retention and go-to-market evidence. The key question for us is whether £750,000 buys enough time and capability to answer those questions rigorously over 18 months. We are keeping the product scope narrow and defining milestones that should materially reduce risk before the next round.”

---

### Objection 2: “Why would a business not just use its accounting software?”
**Honest answer:**  
“Accounting software is part of the current workflow and may already provide useful reporting. Our hypothesis is that many owners still lack a simple, explainable, operational view that combines current bank activity, invoices and accounting data into a near-term cash perspective. We are not assuming that gap exists for every business. We are testing which segments experience it strongly enough to pay for a dedicated layer.”

---

### Objection 3: “Forecasting cash flow is difficult. What if your forecasts are wrong?”
**Honest answer:**  
“It is difficult, and we should not represent forecasts as certainties. Northline’s product needs to show assumptions, data quality and the drivers of a potential gap, rather than produce a black-box number. We intend to measure accuracy and usefulness over time, clearly communicate uncertainty, and design the product as decision support rather than a guarantee.”

---

### Objection 4: “How will you earn customer trust with sensitive financial data?”
**Honest answer:**  
“Trust is central to the product. We will treat security, privacy, access controls, data minimisation, auditability and clear customer permissions as core product requirements. We will obtain appropriate specialist advice and build governance proportionately as the product develops. We will not make security or compliance claims we cannot substantiate.”

---

### Objection 5: “Is Northline regulated?”
**Honest answer:**  
“Northline is intended to be B2B software that does not hold money, lend, make credit decisions or provide investment advice. However, regulatory perimeter can depend on precise product functionality, data access methods, marketing and future features. We will take qualified advice on the scope relevant to our product and maintain clear boundaries. We will not imply authorisation or regulatory status that we do not have.”

---

### Objection 6: “What stops an incumbent or accounting platform from building this?”
**Honest answer:**  
“Incumbents may be able to build overlapping features, so we should not rely on the assumption that they will not. Our initial advantage must come from speed of learning, focus on a specific customer workflow, better usability, trusted integrations, and close customer feedback. Over time, defensibility would need to come from product depth, workflow embedding, data quality, distribution relationships and brand trust—not just the existence of a feature.”

---

### Objection 7: “Your customer segment is too broad: ‘UK micro-businesses’ is not a go-to-market plan.”
**Honest answer:**  
“Agreed. ‘UK micro-businesses’ is our broad problem domain, not our final ICP. We are using the interviews, design partners and pilots to identify a narrower starting segment based on pain intensity, digital readiness, data availability, willingness to pay and ease of reach. We expect the initial customer definition to become more precise as evidence accumulates.”

---

### Objection 8: “Why is £350 per month the right price?”
**Honest answer:**  
“It is an early pilot price, not a fully validated pricing architecture. It gives us evidence that some customers will pay, but it does not yet tell us the optimal price, packaging, gross margin or willingness to pay across segments. We will test pricing alongside product value and onboarding effort.”

---

### Objection 9: “How will you acquire customers without expensive direct sales?”
**Honest answer:**  
“Initially, founder-led sales are appropriate because they generate learning. We are not claiming to have a scalable acquisition channel yet. Once we establish a clear customer profile and repeatable value proposition, we plan to test direct acquisition and selected referral or channel routes, potentially including accountants and bookkeepers. We will measure channel economics before committing heavily.”

---

### Objection 10: “Why will users return regularly?”
**Honest answer:**  
“That is one of the most important unproven questions. We need Northline to become useful in a recurring operating rhythm—such as weekly cash review, invoice follow-up, payment planning or pre-decision checking. We will measure activation, frequency of use, actions taken, customer-reported value, pilot conversion and retention. If use is only occasional, we will need to reconsider the product and pricing model.”

---

### Objection 11: “Two founders are not enough for product, security, sales and support.”
**Honest answer:**  
“The founders cannot do everything indefinitely. The purpose of the round is partly to add carefully selected capacity in engineering, product, security and customer success as evidence justifies it. In the immediate term, we will remain close to customers and use specialist advisers or contractors for areas requiring specific expertise.”

---

### Objection 12: “Why should we believe your market is large enough?”
**Honest answer:**  
“We will not use unsupported top-down market numbers as a substitute for evidence. Our immediate focus is bottom-up: identify a specific customer profile, validate its pain and willingness to pay, understand reachable channels and test retention. If those fundamentals work, we can then make a more defensible case for market expansion.”

## 8. Diligence checklist

Create a clean, indexed data room. Do not wait until a term sheet to organise this.

### A. Corporate and legal
- Certificate of incorporation.
- Articles of association.
- Companies House filings.
- Cap table, fully diluted.
- Details of shares, options, warrants, SAFEs, convertible notes or other rights.
- Board/shareholder consents and minutes, where applicable.
- Founder service agreements or employment agreements.
- Founder IP assignment agreements.
- Contractor agreements and IP assignment clauses.
- Details of any disputes, claims or threatened claims.
- Any grants, loans or tax-relief applications, if applicable.

### B. Fundraising materials
- Current deck.
- One-page investment memo.
- Use-of-funds summary.
- 18-month operating plan.
- Financial model with assumptions clearly identified.
- Proposed round structure, if decided.
- Investor pipeline, kept accurate and confidential.

### C. Product and technology
- Product roadmap.
- Product screenshots, prototype and demo recording.
- Architecture overview.
- List of integrations and their current status.
- Development environment and deployment process.
- Source-code ownership confirmation.
- Repository access and access-control policy.
- Open-source software inventory and licence review.
- Technical debt and known limitations list.
- Uptime, reliability and data-quality measurement plans.
- Disaster recovery and backup approach, proportionate to current stage.

### D. Cybersecurity and data protection
- Data-flow diagram: what data is collected, from whom, where it is processed and who can access it.
- Data inventory and data retention approach.
- Privacy policy and internal privacy documentation.
- Terms of service / customer agreement draft.
- Data processing terms where appropriate.
- Access-control policy.
- Authentication and MFA approach.
- Encryption approach in transit and at rest.
- Secure development practices.
- Incident-response plan.
- Vulnerability-management or testing plan.
- Supplier and subprocesser list.
- Records of security reviews or penetration testing, if conducted.
- Data protection impact assessment where appropriate.
- Evidence of qualified privacy/legal advice where obtained.

Do not claim formal certification, compliance or security standards unless actually achieved.

### E. Regulatory and product-perimeter assessment
- Clear written description of what Northline does and does not do.
- Confirmation that it does not hold funds, lend, make credit decisions or provide investment advice, if accurate.
- Documentation of advice sought regarding relevant UK regulatory perimeter questions.
- Data-access and consent model.
- Marketing-claims review: ensure no statement implies regulated activity, guaranteed financial outcomes or advice.
- Process for reviewing future features before launch.

### F. Customer and commercial evidence
- Customer interview summary:
  - number interviewed;
  - segments;
  - recurring pain points;
  - current workarounds;
  - willingness-to-pay evidence;
  - objections.
- Raw interview notes or anonymised summaries.
- Design partner list and status.
- Paid pilot agreements.

- Customer pipeline, with clear definitions:
  - lead;
  - discovery call;
  - qualified opportunity;
  - pilot;
  - paying customer;
  - churned/paused.

### G. Financials
- Historical management accounts, even if limited.
- Bank statements or accounting exports as appropriate.
- Current cash position.

### H. Team

- References, if requested.

- Hiring plan and salary assumptions.

### I. Commercial and strategic risks
Prepare a one-page risk register covering:

- Data accuracy and incomplete integrations.
- Forecast uncertainty.
- Customer trust and adoption.
- Security and privacy.
- Reliance on third-party platforms or APIs.
- Long onboarding cycles.
- Customer concentration.
- Competitive response from incumbents.
- Regulatory perimeter changes as features evolve.
- Founder capacity and hiring risk.
- Funding/runway risk.

For each risk, show:
- why it matters;
- current mitigation;
- what evidence is still required;
- who owns it.

This is more credible than pretending risks do not exist.

## 9. The investor message to repeat consistently

Use this as the final distilled narrative:

> Northline is building B2B cash-flow intelligence software for UK micro-businesses. It combines invoices, bank-feed data and accounting data to help owners identify likely cash-flow gaps and understand the drivers behind them.  
>

>

The strongest pitch is not “Northline will definitely become the winner in a giant market.” It is:

> “Northline has identified a real, painful workflow problem, has early evidence that customers care, understands what remains unproven, and has a focused plan to convert that uncertainty into evidence.”
