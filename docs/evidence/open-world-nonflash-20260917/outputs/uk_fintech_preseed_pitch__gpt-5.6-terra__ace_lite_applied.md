# Applied Context
- **objective:** Prepare a credible £750k pre-seed fundraising case for Northline without overstating traction, market facts, or regulatory status.
- **audience:** London and European private pre-seed investors familiar with B2B SaaS, fintech infrastructure, and UK SMEs.
- **approach:** Build the story around a specific customer problem, early paid validation, disciplined de-risking, and a clear 18-month use of funds.
- **constraints:** 30 days, £3,000 preparation budget, limited team and evidence, no audited retention or proven product-market fit.
- **deliverable:** Investment narrative, target investor profile, deck, talk track, 30-day plan, objections, and diligence checklist.
- **success check:** Investors should leave with a clear view of the problem, why the team can test the solution, what evidence exists, what remains unproven, and what £750k will validate.

## Reasonable assumptions and validation needs

This assumes Northline is primarily a **cash-flow visibility and forecasting workflow product** for UK micro-businesses—not a lending, payments, investment, or regulated financial-advice product.

Before presenting externally, validate with appropriate UK legal/compliance counsel whether the exact product flows, language, data-access method, data storage, recommendations, and any potential third-party introductions create obligations under applicable data protection, financial-services, or open-banking-related rules. Do not describe the product as “unregulated” unless counsel has confirmed that statement for the actual operating model.

# 1. Investment narrative

## The core narrative

**Northline is building the early-warning layer for cash flow in UK micro-businesses.**

Micro-business owners often learn they have a cash-flow problem too late: after an invoice is delayed, a bank balance is tight, a tax bill is approaching, or supplier payments are due. The relevant data already exists across bank activity, invoices, and accounting systems, but it is fragmented and difficult to translate into a clear, forward-looking answer:

> “When is cash likely to get tight, why, and what should I look at now?”

Northline brings these inputs together to help owners identify likely cash-flow gaps earlier. It is B2B software, designed to improve visibility and decision-making—not to hold money, lend, make credit decisions, or provide investment advice.

The company is early, but it has initial signs worth funding:
- 41 customer interviews informing the problem definition and workflow;
- six unpaid design partners contributing product feedback;
- two paying pilots at **£350 per month**;
- a clickable prototype;
- a founding team combining technical and commercial capability.

The raise is not a claim that the company has already achieved product-market fit. It is a focused request for capital to answer the next set of investable questions:

1. Can Northline turn the prototype into a secure, reliable product that customers use repeatedly?
2. Can it convert design-partner learning into a repeatable customer workflow and clear ideal customer profile?
3. Can it establish early retention, willingness to pay, acquisition, and product-engagement evidence?
4. Can it build the foundations required to handle sensitive financial data responsibly?

## The investment case in one paragraph

Northline is an early B2B software company addressing a recurring and emotionally important operating problem: micro-business owners need earlier visibility into likely cash shortfalls. The founders have moved beyond a concept through 41 interviews, six design partners, two paid pilots, and a prototype. They are raising £750k to build and validate a secure product, prove a repeatable wedge in a defined customer segment, and generate the retention and commercial evidence required for the next financing decision. The opportunity is not yet fully proven; the investment is a structured bet on a high-frequency customer problem, an informed early team, and a disciplined plan to reduce product, security, and go-to-market risk.

## Positioning language to use

Use:
- “Cash-flow visibility and early-warning software”
- “A forward-looking view built from the business’s existing financial data”
- “Decision-support software for business owners”
- “Helping owners see potential pressure points earlier”
- “Designed to complement existing accounting workflows”
- “Testing a repeatable workflow with early customers”

Avoid:
- “We predict the future”
- “We guarantee businesses won’t run out of cash”
- “AI-powered financial advice” unless this is both true and carefully supportable
- “The only platform…” unless independently verified
- “Regulated fintech” or “unregulated” without legal confirmation
- “We will replace accountants/accounting platforms”
- “We have product-market fit”
- “We can save every business £X” without robust evidence

# 2. Investor profile to target

## Best-fit investor characteristics

Prioritise private pre-seed investors who:
- invest at **pre-seed or first institutional cheque** stage;
- are comfortable funding evidence creation, not only scaled metrics;
- understand B2B SaaS, fintech software, accounting technology, or SME tools;
- can support a £750k round, either as lead, co-lead, or active syndicate participant;
- have relevant networks among SME operators, accountants, bookkeepers, fintech infrastructure providers, and later-stage seed investors;
- are comfortable with UK and European venture structures and UK data/security expectations;
- value candid founder communication and clear milestones.

## Priority investor categories

| Investor type | Why it may fit | What to seek |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed fintech or B2B SaaS funds | Familiar with long validation cycles, integrations, security, and workflow software | A lead or high-conviction anchor |
| Operator angels | Can provide practical feedback on SME distribution, finance teams, sales, and product | Early cheques, introductions, commercial judgment |
| Fintech founders and executives | Understand product trust, data access, customer acquisition, and regulated-adjacent boundaries | Credibility, advice, selective customer or hiring introductions |
| Accounting-tech / SME-software operators | May understand the actual user and channel dynamics | Design-partner insight and channel validation |
| Angel syndicates or early-stage networks | Useful for filling a round after a credible lead develops conviction | Efficient access to multiple aligned investors |
| Family offices with early-stage technology appetite | Potentially patient capital if they understand the risk profile | Flexible participation, but assess decision speed |

## Deprioritise initially

Avoid spending major time on:
- later-stage growth funds expecting material ARR, retention cohorts, and efficient growth;
- generalist investors who require a broad consumer-style market story rather than evidence of a workflow;
- investors whose portfolio creates a direct conflict with a close competing product;
- investors who expect financial-services licensing or lending economics as the core thesis, if that is not Northline’s model;
- investors outside the UK/Europe who cannot readily support UK company, data, or market context.

## Outreach targeting criteria

Build a list of approximately **60–90 high-fit people**, not 300 weak leads. Score each on:
1. Stage fit;
2. Sector relevance;
3. Likely cheque-size fit;
4. Evidence of investing in UK/European companies;
5. Value beyond capital;
6. Warm-introduction path;
7. Portfolio conflicts.

Aim for a process with a concentrated first wave: roughly **25–35 priority conversations**, then expand based on feedback and momentum.

# 4. Five-minute talk track

## 0:00–0:30 — Opening

“Northline is building cash-flow early-warning software for UK micro-businesses. Owners often have the underlying data—bank activity, invoices, and accounting records—but not a clear, forward-looking picture of when cash may become tight. We help bring that information together so owners can spot likely gaps earlier and understand what is driving them.”

## 0:30–1:05 — Problem

“For many micro-businesses, cash-flow management is reactive. The bank balance tells them where they are today, accounting tools may record what has happened, and invoices show money that may arrive—but turning all of that into a practical view of the weeks ahead often requires manual work, spreadsheets, and judgment.

“The consequence is not necessarily a lack of data. It is a lack of timely clarity. Owners need to know: where could pressure emerge, how confident should I be in the forecast, and what should I investigate now?”

## 1:05–1:40 — Product and scope

“Northline is designed as B2B software for that workflow. It brings together relevant invoice, bank-feed, and accounting-software data to help surface likely cash-flow gaps and their drivers.

“We are deliberately clear about what Northline is not. We do not hold customer money, lend, make credit decisions, or give investment advice. Our focus is better visibility and operational decision support.”

## 1:40–2:20 — Evidence so far

“We are early, and we do not claim product-market fit. But we have moved beyond a purely theoretical idea.

“We have completed 41 customer interviews, are working with six unpaid design partners, have two paid pilots at £350 per month, and have developed a clickable prototype. The interviews and pilots have helped us narrow the problem and identify the workflow we need to test in a real product.

“The paid pilots are encouraging, but the purpose of this round is to establish whether adoption, retention, pricing, and distribution can become repeatable.”

## 2:20–3:05 — Customer wedge and go-to-market

“Our near-term approach is intentionally narrow. Rather than claiming to serve every micro-business, we will focus on the segment where payment timing, invoice visibility, and existing data tools make the problem most acute and the product easiest to validate.

“We will begin with founder-led sales and structured onboarding. That lets us learn quickly: which customers activate, what they use weekly, which alerts they trust, what they are prepared to pay for, and why they stay or leave. We will also test whether accountants and bookkeepers can become an effective channel, but we will not assume that before evidence.”

## 3:05–3:45 — Why this team and why funding now

“We have a technical cofounder able to lead product delivery and a commercial founder focused on customer development and go-to-market. We are raising now because the key risks are clear and actionable: build a secure product, validate data and workflow reliability, and convert early customer learning into real cohort evidence.”

## 3:45–4:30 — Raise and milestones

“We are raising £750,000 to fund 18 months of product, security, and go-to-market work. The capital will go principally toward product and engineering, security and privacy foundations, and customer acquisition and success.

“At the end of that period, we intend to have a functioning and responsibly operated product, a defined initial customer segment, evidence on engagement and retention, a better-understood acquisition motion, and a much clearer basis for a scale round—or for making a disciplined decision if the evidence does not support scaling.”

## 4:30–5:00 — Closing

“This is a pre-seed investment in a focused customer problem and a disciplined de-risking plan, not a claim that every commercial question has already been answered. We are looking for investors who understand B2B SaaS and fintech-adjacent software, and who can help us turn promising early signals into a repeatable company.”

# 5. 30-day evidence and outreach plan

## Operating principles

- Spend the £3,000 on evidence and presentation quality—not vanity branding or paid lead generation.
- Keep customer research, fundraising materials, product claims, and compliance statements consistent.
- Track every investor conversation and objection in one system.
- Ask for introductions only after the narrative, deck, and data room are credible.
- Do not wait for perfection. Launch outreach once core materials are ready, then improve weekly.

## Suggested budget

| Item | Indicative budget | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Legal/compliance scoping consultation | £800–£1,200 | Validate product-boundary claims, data/privacy questions, and wording |
| Deck design / editing support | £500–£900 | Improve clarity and visual credibility, not elaborate branding |
| Customer-research incentives | £400–£700 | Secure focused follow-up interviews or usability sessions |
| Security/privacy baseline review or specialist consultation | £500–£900 | Identify immediate gaps and create a credible remediation plan |
| Data room, tools, contingency | £200–£400 | Document organisation, meeting tools, minor expenses |

Actual allocation should follow existing access to legal, design, and security expertise.

## Days 1–5: establish the factual base

**Outputs**
- One-page company fact sheet;
- Investor narrative and deck v1;
- Customer evidence log;
- Draft 18-month operating plan and cash model;
- Initial data room structure;
- A list of product, legal, data, and security claims requiring validation.

**Actions**
1. Create a customer-research summary:
   - interview profile;
   - method;
   - recurring problems;
   - customer language;
   - counterevidence and objections;
   - unknowns.
2. Interview the two paid pilots deeply:
   - why did they agree to pay?
   - what job were they hiring Northline to do?
   - what is currently valuable vs merely interesting?
   - how often do they expect to use it?
   - what would make them cancel?
   - what would make £350/month clearly worthwhile?
3. Produce an 18-month monthly cash plan:
   - founders;
   - contractors/employees;
   - cloud/software;
   - security/legal/compliance;
   - sales/customer success;
   - contingency.
4. Define 5–8 internal success metrics for the next 18 months, such as:
   - activated accounts;
   - time to first value;
   - weekly/monthly active use;
   - forecast/alert review behaviour;
   - pilot-to-paid conversion;
   - logo retention;
   - revenue retention;
   - sales-cycle length.

## Days 6–10: convert learning into evidence

**Outputs**
- Deck v2;
- Prototype/usability findings;
- Clear initial ICP hypothesis;
- Product roadmap with current vs planned features;
- Draft information-security and privacy posture summary.

**Actions**
1. Run **8–12 targeted follow-up interviews or usability sessions**, prioritising the proposed initial ICP.
2. Test specific messages:
   - “What is the value of earlier warning?”
   - “What data would you be comfortable connecting?”
   - “Would you trust an alert? What evidence would it need?”
   - “Who owns this decision: founder, finance lead, accountant, bookkeeper?”
   - “What pricing structure feels plausible?”
3. Build a decision log separating:
   - validated;
   - supported but incomplete;
   - untested;
   - disproven.
4. Obtain legal/compliance advice focused on:
   - precise product descriptions;
   - data-access and consent flows;
   - privacy and data-processing implications;
   - marketing wording;
   - any perimeter risks created by recommendations, referrals, or future product features.
5. Draft a concise risk register and remediation roadmap.

## Days 11–15: prepare an investor-grade package

**Outputs**
- Final core deck;
- One-page teaser;
- Financial model;
- Use-of-funds page;
- Data room v1;
- Outreach list with warm paths;
- Email and meeting scripts.

**Actions**
1. Build a 60–90 person investor list and rank the top 30.
2. Ask founders, advisers, design partners, and trusted operators for a limited number of specific warm introductions.
3. Prepare a consistent answer sheet for:
   - market;
   - competition;
   - regulatory boundaries;
   - security;
   - pilots;
   - pricing;
   - use of funds;
   - valuation/round structure, if decided.
4. Hold 3–5 pitch rehearsals with experienced founders/operators. Ask them to challenge every unsupported claim.
5. Set data-room access controls and ensure no customer-confidential data is shared without permission.

## Days 16–23: launch outreach and learn rapidly

**Outputs**
- Active pipeline;
- Investor feedback log;
- Deck v3 based on repeated feedback;
- Updated FAQ.

**Actions**
1. Start with the highest-fit warm introductions.
2. Send a short, specific outreach note:
   - what Northline does;
   - current evidence;
   - amount being raised;
   - why the recipient is a fit;
   - request for a 20–30 minute conversation.
3. Target a dense meeting period rather than spreading meetings over months.
4. After every meeting, record:
   - core objection;
   - evidence requested;
   - whether the investor sees a lead path;
   - useful introductions;
   - follow-up deadline.
5. Do not treat every objection as a request to change the story. Look for repeated patterns across multiple informed investors.

## Days 24–30: create momentum through evidence, not pressure

**Outputs**
- Updated pipeline and next-step schedule;
- Refined deck and data room;
- Clear round strategy;
- End-of-month investor update.

**Actions**
1. Re-engage investors who requested milestones or materials.
2. Share meaningful updates only:
   - a new paid customer;
   - documented design-partner conversion;
   - verified usability results;
   - security plan completion;
   - a material product milestone.
3. Ask strong early supporters to introduce one or two genuinely relevant investors.
4. Decide whether to:
   - continue toward a priced round;
   - use an appropriate early-stage convertible instrument, subject to legal/tax advice;
   - extend customer-validation work before formally launching a full raise.

Avoid artificial deadlines or invented competitive interest.

# 7. Diligence checklist

Organise this as a clean, permission-controlled data room. Label documents clearly, date them, and maintain a version log.

## A. Corporate, founders, and financing

- Certificate of incorporation and constitutional documents;
- Cap table, including founder ownership, options, convertibles, warrants, and any promised equity;
- Shareholder agreements and investor rights, if any;
- Board/shareholder consents and material resolutions;
- Founder employment/service agreements;
- IP assignment agreements from each founder, contractor, and relevant adviser;
- Details of any prior grants, loans, liabilities, or commitments;
- Proposed financing structure and draft terms when available;
- Confirmation of who is authorised to negotiate and sign.

## B. Product and technology

- Product overview: what exists, what is in prototype, and what is planned;
- Product roadmap linked to customer and business milestones;
- System architecture diagram;
- Description of each data source and integration approach;
- Code repository ownership and access controls;
- Development process and release controls;
- Third-party software/services inventory;
- Key dependencies, service-level risks, and contingency plans;
- Technical debt and known limitations log;
- Uptime, incident, or performance information if the product is live;
- Documentation of how forecasts/alerts are generated, including assumptions and limitations.

## C. Security, privacy, and data governance

- Data map: what data is collected, where it comes from, why it is used, where it is stored, who can access it, and retention/deletion approach;
- Privacy notice and customer terms, in draft or final form;
- Data-processing agreements and relevant vendor terms;
- Information-security policy set, proportionate to stage;
- Access-control policy, authentication approach, and privileged-access management;
- Encryption approach for data in transit and at rest, where applicable;
- Secure development and vulnerability-management process;
- Incident-response plan;
- Backup and disaster-recovery approach;
- Penetration-test plan or results, if performed;
- Record of security risks and remediation timeline;
- Data-protection impact assessment or legal advice where appropriate;
- Documentation of consent and permissions flows for connected data.

Do not claim certifications, audits, or compliance standards that have not been completed.

## D. Regulatory and legal boundary

- Written description of product scope and excluded activities;
- Legal advice or issue list covering relevant financial-services and data/privacy questions;
- Assessment of whether any permissions, registrations, contracts, disclosures, or third-party arrangements are required;
- Marketing-review process to avoid misleading claims;
- Complaint-handling and customer-support process;
- Insurance position and plan, where relevant;
- Record of any regulatory correspondence, if any.

## E. Commercial evidence

- Interview-research methodology and anonymised findings;
- Design-partner list, status, agreements, and permission to reference;
- Pilot agreements, invoices, payment confirmation, and scope;
- Pipeline list with stage definitions;
- Customer personas and ICP hypothesis;
- Pricing hypotheses and pilot learnings;
- Sales process and sales-cycle observations;
- Customer onboarding process;
- Usage and retention definitions, even if data is limited;
- Churn/cancellation reasons, if any;
- Customer testimonials only where written permission exists;
- Competitive landscape notes clearly labelled as internal working hypotheses unless externally verified.

## F. Financials and operating plan

- Historical management accounts, even if limited;
- Bank statements or accounting records appropriate for diligence;
- Current monthly burn and runway;
- 18-month monthly cash-flow forecast;
- Headcount and contractor plan;
- Use-of-funds breakdown;
- Base, downside, and delayed-revenue scenarios;
- Material commitments and future liabilities;
- Assumptions register identifying what is evidence-backed versus estimated.

## G. Team and hiring

- Founder biographies and relevant experience;
- Organisation chart, current and planned;
- Hiring plan and compensation assumptions;
- Adviser list, roles, and any equity arrangements;
- Background/reference materials where appropriate;
- Key-person dependency risks and mitigations.

## H. Fundraising materials and claims audit

Before sharing externally, review every deck statement against one of these labels:
- **Fact:** supported by an internal record or reliable external source;
- **Customer evidence:** based on interviews/pilots, with method and sample known;
- **Hypothesis:** plausible but unproven;
- **Target:** future goal, not a forecast or result;
- **Legal/compliance position:** confirmed by appropriate advice or explicitly still under review.

This discipline will make Northline more credible than a deck built around aggressive but fragile claims.
