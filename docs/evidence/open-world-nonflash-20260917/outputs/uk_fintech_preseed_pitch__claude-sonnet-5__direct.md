# Northline Investor Pitch Package

## 1. Investment Narrative

**Core thesis:** UK micro-businesses run on spreadsheets and guesswork for cash-flow visibility. The data to fix this already exists — scattered across invoicing tools, bank feeds, and accounting software. Northline's wedge is integration and clarity, not lending or credit decisions, which keeps the regulatory footprint light and the build focused.

**Why now:** Open banking infrastructure is mature enough to build on reliably; accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent) has normalised API access; micro-business owners are increasingly comfortable connecting financial tools. You're not evangelising a new behaviour — you're removing manual reconciliation work owners already do badly in spreadsheets.

**Why you, why now:** Two complementary founders, six design partners actively using the product, two businesses paying for it before you've raised a pound, and 41 structured conversations that shaped the roadmap. This is disciplined, capital-efficient validation — not spray-and-pray building.

**What the money is for:** 18 months to convert design-partner insight into a defensible, secure product with real go-to-market traction — not to chase growth metrics prematurely.

**What you are NOT claiming:** product-market fit, defensible retention, regulatory authorisation, or a moat. You are claiming a well-scoped problem, disciplined early validation, and a credible plan to earn the next round on evidence.

---

## 2. Investor Profile to Target

**Best fit:**
- **Pre-seed/seed generalist funds** with UK fintech infrastructure experience (not consumer lending or neobank-focused — you're B2B SaaS, not a lender)
- **Angels who are ex-operators** from Xero, FreeAgent, Sage, GoCardless, Tide — they'll understand the integration challenge and won't ask you to "just get a lending licence"
- **B2B SaaS-focused seed funds** who evaluate on usage/retention signals rather than requiring regulatory moats
- Funds explicitly comfortable with **pre-PMF, evidence-stage** investing (many pre-seed funds say this but actually want more traction — filter hard)

**Avoid or deprioritise:**
- Funds whose portfolio is dominated by regulated lenders/BNPL — they'll misjudge your risk profile or push you toward lending features you don't want
- Growth-stage or Series A funds masquerading as "flexible stage" — wrong check size, wrong diligence bar
- Anyone who needs "10x return in 3 years" language from you at this stage — mismatched expectations

**Practical sourcing:** Warm intros via your design partners' networks, Xero/QuickBooks app marketplace ecosystem events, UK fintech-focused angel syndicates (e.g., syndicates on Odin/Vauban rather than cold platform blasts).

## 3. Ten-Slide Deck Outline

1. **Title + one-line problem** — "Micro-businesses can't see cash-flow gaps coming until it's too late."
2. **The problem, grounded in your interviews** — quote 2-3 real pain points from the 41 interviews (anonymised, specific, not generic stats)
3. **Why this is hard to solve today** — fragmented data across invoicing/banking/accounting, manual reconciliation, no single source of truth
4. **The product** — clickable prototype screenshots, what it actually does (aggregates, visualises, forecasts gaps — does NOT lend, hold money, or advise)
5. **What we've learned and built** — 6 design partners, their engagement, what changed in the product because of them
6. **Early commercial signal** — 2 paying pilots at £350/mo, framed honestly as "first proof buyers will pay," not "traction"
7. **Market context** (only what you can source/cite — e.g., publicly available ONS figures on UK micro-business count; do NOT invent TAM figures)
8. **Business model** — pricing logic, path from pilot pricing to scaled pricing, why B2B SaaS economics work here
9. **Team** — technical + commercial founder, why this pairing, relevant prior experience
10. **The ask** — £750k, 18-month runway, three or four named milestones this money buys (see below), use of funds breakdown

## 4. Five-Minute Talk Track

**[0:00–0:40] Open with the problem, not the company.**
"Every micro-business owner we've spoken to — 41 of them now — manages cash flow the same way: a spreadsheet, a gut feeling, and a scramble when a big invoice doesn't land on time. The data to prevent that surprise already exists. It's just scattered across three systems that don't talk to each other."

**[0:40–1:30] The gap and why it persists.**
"Bank feeds tell you what's happened. Invoicing tools tell you what's owed. Accounting software tells you what's booked. None of them tell you: 'in 11 days, you won't be able to make payroll.' Building that view means integrating three data sources cleanly and translating it into something a non-finance owner actually understands — that's the hard part, and it's why it hasn't been solved well for this segment."

**[1:30–2:30] What we've built and validated.**
"We built a clickable prototype and put it in front of six design partners who use it regularly, and two businesses are paying us £350 a month for it today — before we've raised any outside capital. We've deliberately kept scope tight: we don't hold money, we don't lend, we don't make credit decisions. We show you the gap coming. That's it. That focus is what let us get this far on almost no budget."

**[2:30–3:30] Honest state of play.**
"We want to be direct about where we are: this is early. We have signal, not proof. We don't have retention data yet, we're not FCA-authorised because our current product doesn't require it, and we're not going to tell you we've found product-market fit — we haven't, yet. What we have is a sharply scoped problem, real payer validation, and a founding team that's spent the last months listening more than building."

**[3:30–4:30] The ask and the plan.**
"We're raising £750,000 for 18 months. That funds three things: hardening the product and its security posture, converting design partners and pilots into a repeatable paid cohort, and building the go-to-market motion to get from two customers to a validated, repeatable pipeline. We'll know in 18 months, with evidence, whether this is a venture-scale business — and we want investors who are comfortable backing that evidence-gathering stage, not ones who need us to claim we're already there."

**[4:30–5:00] Close.**
"We're not asking you to believe in a market size slide. We're asking you to believe that two founders who've done 41 interviews and got two people to pay before raising money are the right team to go find out, properly, whether this is real. We'd like you to be part of finding that out with us."

## 5. 30-Day Evidence & Outreach Plan (£3,000 budget)

**Days 1–5: Sharpen the evidence base**
- Extract 5–8 strong, specific verbatim quotes from the 41 interviews (with permission) — these are more credible than any invented stat
- Get simple usage data from your 6 design partners: login frequency, which feature they touch most, any moment where the product changed a decision they made
- Document the two paying pilots' actual use case in one page each — what pain, what triggered the purchase, what nearly stopped them
- Budget: £0 (founder time)

**Days 6–12: Tighten the product story**
- Polish the clickable prototype for a 90-second live or recorded walkthrough — no fake data claims, use real (anonymised) pilot scenarios
- Get one design partner or pilot customer to agree to a short video testimonial or written quote for the deck
- Budget: ~£500 (video editing/freelance help if needed)

**Days 13–18: Build target investor list and warm paths**
- Build a list of 25–35 investors matching the profile above (name-specific, not "seed funds")
- Map warm intro paths through design partners, pilot customers, existing network, portfolio-company founders
- Draft a tight one-page teaser (not the full deck) for intro requests
- Budget: ~£300 (Crunchbase/other data tool short subscription if needed)

**Days 19–24: Rehearse and pressure-test**
- Run the 5-minute talk track live with 3–5 friendly but critical people (ideally including someone with fintech investing experience) — ask them to attack every claim
- Prepare written answers to the objections section below; rehearse delivering them without defensiveness
- Legal/company-secretary check: confirm cap table, SEIS/EIS advance assurance status (this matters a lot to UK angels — get this in motion now if not already done)
- Budget: ~£1,200 (advisory/legal hours for SEIS/EIS advance assurance application if not started)

**Days 25–30: Launch outreach**
- Send warm intro requests in batches of 5–8, not all at once (allows you to improve pitch between rounds)
- Track every meeting: what question stumped you, what claim someone doubted — feed this back into the deck weekly
- Budget: ~£1,000 buffer for travel to in-person meetings (London-focused)

**Total: ~£3,000**

## 6. Likely Hard Objections and Honest Answers

**"You have no product-market fit. How do I know anyone will pay for this at scale?"**
*"We don't know that yet, honestly. What we have is two businesses paying £350/month before any sales process, and six design partners still actively engaged after months of no pressure to stay. That's early signal, not proof. This round is explicitly to go find that proof — if we already had it, we'd be raising a much larger round at a much higher price."*

**"Two paying pilots and no retention data — why should I believe this holds up?"**
*"You're right to flag it — we don't have retention data because we haven't had customers long enough to generate meaningful data. We'd rather tell you that plainly than dress up two months of payments as a retention curve. Part of what this raise funds is getting to a cohort size and time horizon where retention data actually means something."*

**"What stops Xero or QuickBooks building this natively and killing you?"**
*"Nothing structurally stops them, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. Our bet is that platform owners are incentivized to stay neutral infrastructure and are slow-moving on opinionated forecasting features, and that a focused product can move faster and get closer to this specific pain than a platform feature ever will. That's a real risk, not a solved one — we'd want to keep proving speed and specificity as our edge."*

**"Are you regulated? What happens when you inevitably want to add lending or advice features?"**
*"We are not FCA-authorised, and we don't need to be for what we currently do — we aggregate and visualise data; we don't hold funds, lend, or advise. If we ever moved toward credit or advice features, that would trigger a real regulatory process, real cost, and real time, and we'd only do that deliberately, with proper legal advice, not as a roadmap afterthought."*

**"Why should a first check be £750k rather than a smaller amount to prove more first?"**
*"That's a fair challenge on sizing. This number reflects two technical/product hires, security work we consider non-negotiable given we handle financial data, and enough go-to-market runway to get a real signal rather than a rushed one. We're open to discussing structure — including staged tranches tied to milestones — if that better matches your risk appetite."*

**"What's your unfair advantage — why you two, why now?"**
*"Bluntly: at pre-seed, unfair advantage is thin for anyone. What we'd point to is discipline — we ran 41 structured interviews before writing a product spec, we got paying customers before raising money, and we've kept scope narrow rather than chasing a bigger, vaguer vision. That's not a moat, but it's evidence of how we'll operate with your capital."*

## 7. Diligence Checklist (What to Have Ready)

**Legal/corporate**
- Cap table (current and pro forma post-raise)
- Articles of association, shareholder agreements
- SEIS/EIS advance assurance status
- IP assignment agreements from both founders (and any contractors)
- Any existing SAFE/convertible instruments or side letters

**Commercial**
- Signed pilot agreements (£350/month customers) — actual contracts, not verbal
- Design partner agreements or MOUs, even informal
- Interview notes/summary from the 41 conversations (anonymised, organised)
- Pipeline list of prospects beyond current pilots

**Product/technical**
- Architecture overview — what's built vs. prototype vs. planned
- Data handling approach: what customer financial data touches your systems, how it's stored, third-party APIs relied on (open banking provider, accounting software APIs)
- Security posture honestly stated: what exists today vs. what the raise will fund (pen testing, ISO27001 path, etc. — don't claim certifications you don't have)

**Financial**
- Current burn rate and runway without this raise
- Use-of-funds breakdown for the £750k (headcount, security/compliance spend, go-to-market spend, buffer)
- 18-month milestone plan tied to funding tranches if staged

**Team**
- Founder backgrounds, CVs, equity split rationale
- Vesting schedules
- Any advisors formally engaged (with terms, not just names on a slide)

---

**One last honest note:** the strongest thing in this whole pitch is your discipline — narrow scope, real interviews, real (if small) paying customers, no inflated claims. Investors who do fintech pre-seed well will read that discipline as the actual signal, more than any single metric. Lead with it, don't apologize for the stage you're at.
