## 1. Positioning: be narrower than “AI for freelancers”

Your strongest positioning is not “learn AI content creation.” That is crowded and vague.

Use this instead:

> **I help independent B2B designers turn the real thinking already inside their projects into a consistent, responsible AI-assisted content system—without outsourcing their judgment or sounding like everyone else.**

More specific version for your first 90 days:

> **For independent brand and web designers in Spain who serve B2B clients, I help turn approved project work, client questions, and design decisions into LinkedIn posts, short videos, newsletters, and simple offers using AI as an assistant—not a replacement for expertise.**

Why start with independent designers:

- You already understand their work and language.
- They have visual evidence, project stories, process decisions, and client education material.
- Their work is often expensive but invisible between portfolio updates.
- They are likely to care about voice, originality, ethics, and client confidentiality.
- A designer who gets results is a credible referral source to copywriters and consultants later.

Do not target Spain, Portugal, the whole EU, designers, copywriters, and consultants equally at first. For 90 days:

- **Primary market:** independent brand/web designers in Spain.
- **Primary language:** Spanish.
- **Secondary access language:** English for EU-based designers and Portuguese freelancers comfortable working in English.
- **Expansion later:** copywriters, consultants, and small independent studios.

You can remain bilingual without creating twice as much work. Publish mostly in Spanish for this first niche, with selected English posts and an English version of your landing page/product.

## 2. Your offer ladder

The ladder should move people from “I need a clearer system” to “I want help installing it.”

### Free entry point: The Proof-to-Post Map

A short downloadable worksheet or email mini-series:

**“The Proof-to-Post Map: 10 places to find content ideas inside your existing client work.”**

It should help someone identify:

- approved project decisions
- common client questions
- before/after reasoning
- mistakes they see in their field
- principles they use in their work
- useful opinions they can defend
- things AI can assist with vs. things only they should decide

This is not a generic “100 ChatGPT prompts” lead magnet. Its purpose is to get the right people to see that they already have useful source material.

CTA: reply with “MAP,” download it, or join the waitlist for the next live sprint.

### Offer 1: Low-cost template product

## The Responsible Content System for Independent Designers  
**Beta price: €29**  
**Standard price after validation: €39–49**

A bilingual Notion or Google Docs-based template kit. Keep it small and useful.

Include:

1. **Source Bank**
   - Capture project decisions, client questions, observations, and ideas.
2. **Voice and Boundaries Sheet**
   - What you sound like, what you refuse to say, phrases you use, claims you will not make.
3. **Claim-Checking Checklist**
   - “Is this true?” “Can I show evidence?” “Does this expose client information?” “Did AI invent a claim?”
4. **Content Brief Template**
   - Audience, source material, point of view, proof, CTA, format.
5. **Prompt Patterns**
   - Prompts that use the creator’s own sources and constraints.
   - Not “write 30 viral posts.”
6. **LinkedIn post, short video, and newsletter frameworks**
7. **30-day publishing planner**
8. **A 20-minute walkthrough video**

The promise:

> Build a repeatable system for turning your actual work into useful content without using AI to invent expertise, imitate other people, or publish generic filler.

Do not make this a giant library. A small tool that gets used is more valuable than 200 prompts.

### Offer 2: Live cohort

## Proof-to-Post Sprint  
**Beta cohort: €295**  
**Standard price: €395–450**  
**Capacity: 6 people**  
**Format: 3 weeks, 90-minute live session each week**

This is your core offer. It should be implementation-focused, not a lecture series.

By the end, each participant leaves with:

- a source bank built from their real work
- a clear voice and AI-use boundary document
- 4 LinkedIn posts ready to publish
- 1 useful newsletter outline or draft
- 2 short video scripts
- a 30-day content plan
- a responsible AI workflow they can repeat themselves

### Suggested curriculum

**Week 1: Find the proof**  
Extract material from client work, project decisions, questions, methods, and opinions. Set privacy, permission, and fact-checking boundaries.

**Week 2: Turn proof into content**  
Use AI for structuring, reframing, outlining, and editing—not for fabricating expertise. Build LinkedIn, video, and newsletter assets.

**Week 3: Build the system**  
Create a light publishing rhythm, feedback loop, source capture habit, and offer CTA.

Include a small amount of async feedback, but do not promise unlimited review. You only have ten hours per week.

A real reason for the six-person cap:

> Six seats because every participant gets direct feedback on their source material and content system.

That is legitimate capacity-based scarcity, not fake urgency.

Set a minimum viable cohort of four paid people. If you do not reach four, offer people a refund or a rescheduled date. State this clearly before purchase.

## 3. The realistic path to €3,000/month

Treat €3,000 as a **monthly revenue run-rate target**, not a promise for the first 90 days.

A sustainable model within ten hours per week could look like this:

| Revenue source | Volume | Price | Monthly average |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cohort every 6 weeks | 6 participants / 1.5 months | €395 | €1,580 |
| Premium implementation | 1 client/month | €1,250 | €1,250 |
| Template product | 5 sales/month | €39 | €195 |
| **Total** |  |  | **€3,025/month** |

This is not easy, but it is plausible because the cohort produces the strongest learning, proof, testimonials, and upsell opportunities.

Note:

- Cohort revenue will arrive unevenly: for example, €2,370 when a six-seat cohort fills rather than exactly €1,580 every month.
- This is revenue before payment processing fees, software costs, taxes, VAT obligations, and your personal income.
- Do not try to run weekly cohorts. A cohort every six weeks is a better fit for your time and audience size.

Your first 90-day target is not “hit €3,000 every month.” It is:

1. validate a specific audience/problem,
2. get 6 people through a paid beta cohort,
3. sell 1 implementation pilot,
4. get real proof and language from customers,
5. create a repeatable enrollment process.

A reasonable 90-day sales target:

| Item | Target | Beta price | Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Template product | 8 sales | €29 | €232 |
| Beta cohort | 6 seats | €295 | €1,770 |
| Premium pilot | 1 client | €900 | €900 |
| **Total possible collected revenue** |  |  | **€2,902** |

That is a useful validation target without pretending success is guaranteed.

## 4. One repeatable content series for LinkedIn, email, and YouTube

Use one series, not separate content strategies for every platform.

## Series: “From Proof to Post”

Each episode starts with a real piece of source material from freelance work:

- a client question
- a project decision
- a portfolio case study
- a proposal objection
- a design principle
- a mistake you made
- a useful opinion you can defend
- an anonymized lesson from client work

Then show how it becomes content responsibly.

### Episode structure

1. **The source**
   - “A client asked me why we should not start with a moodboard.”
2. **The human judgment**
   - What you believe, what you noticed, what evidence supports it.
3. **What AI can help with**
   - Organizing notes, offering angles, turning an outline into formats, spotting gaps.
4. **What AI cannot decide**
   - Your opinion, whether a claim is true, whether confidential information is safe to share, whether the advice is appropriate.
5. **The output**
   - One LinkedIn post angle, one short video idea, one newsletter angle.
6. **The lesson**
   - A reusable principle.
7. **The CTA**
   - Download the Proof-to-Post Map, reply with a keyword, buy the template, or join the next cohort.

### One weekly source becomes:

| Channel | Output |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn post 1 | Story or opinion: the project decision and what it taught you |
| LinkedIn post 2 | Practical breakdown: source → AI-assisted process → final content |
| LinkedIn post 3 | A specific lesson, objection response, or invitation to the cohort |
| Email | Deeper version with a personal note, framework, or behind-the-scenes reflection |
| YouTube | 6–10 minute screen-share walkthrough showing the process |
| Optional short clip | 30–60 second clip from the YouTube walkthrough |

You do not need daily posting. A sustainable rhythm is better:

- **3 LinkedIn posts per week**
- **1 email per week**
- **1 simple YouTube video per week**, or every other week if weekly video compromises sales and delivery

### Language approach

For the first 90 days:

- Write two LinkedIn posts per week in Spanish.
- Write one in English when the topic is relevant to EU freelancers or your English-speaking audience.
- Send subscribers an email in their chosen language where possible. If you cannot segment yet, keep the email primarily Spanish and include a short English summary/link.
- Make the product and key landing page bilingual.

Do not translate every asset perfectly. That will consume your limited time. Adapt the best-performing ideas into the other language.

## 5. Your 90-day plan

## Days 1–14: Narrow, listen, and set up the minimum

### Objective
Confirm that independent designers have an urgent version of this problem and learn their actual language.

### Actions

1. Create a simple one-page positioning page:
   - who it is for
   - the problem
   - your ethical AI approach
   - a waitlist for the Proof-to-Post Sprint
   - a link to book a 20-minute research conversation

2. Invite 20–30 relevant people personally:
   - existing LinkedIn contacts
   - former collaborators
   - designers in Barcelona, Madrid, Valencia, Lisbon, and relevant EU communities
   - email subscribers who identify as designers

Use a human message, not a mass pitch:

> I’m developing a small live sprint for independent designers who want to turn project experience into useful content with AI, without sounding generic or sharing client-sensitive material. I’m not selling anything on this call. Could I ask you 6 questions about how you currently market your work?

3. Conduct 10–12 research calls.

Ask:
- What makes marketing inconsistent for you?
- What have you tried with AI?
- What did you dislike about the result?
- What source material do you already have?
- What would make content creation feel worth doing?
- What result would be worth paying for?
- What words would you use to describe the problem?

4. Publish four pieces from the “From Proof to Post” series.

5. Build the basic lead magnet and delivery stack:
   - landing page: Carrd or similar
   - form: Tally
   - email: your existing platform
   - delivery: Notion/Google Drive
   - payment: Stripe/Gumroad/Lemon Squeezy, depending on your tax setup

### Success criteria by day 14

- 10 research conversations completed
- 15–25 waitlist subscribers
- At least 5 people describe essentially the same pain:
  - “I have ideas, but no system.”
  - “AI makes me sound generic.”
  - “I don’t know what I can share from client work.”
  - “I am inconsistent because I overthink every post.”

If you hear a different, stronger pain repeatedly, adjust the offer around that.

## 8. Your weekly review rhythm

Schedule a 45-minute review every Friday.

### Review these five questions

1. **Who engaged?**  
   Which designers, not which posts, created meaningful replies, calls, signups, or purchases?

2. **What language worked?**  
   Note the exact phrases people use about their problem. Use their language in your next post and landing page.

3. **Where is the bottleneck?**  
   - Not enough qualified visitors? Improve distribution and direct outreach.
   - Visitors but no signups? Improve the lead magnet and landing page.
   - Signups but no purchases? Improve the offer, proof, fit, or sales conversation.
   - Buyers but low completion? Improve onboarding and product usability.

4. **What is taking too much time?**  
   Cut or simplify it. Your system must fit ten hours weekly.

5. **What is next week’s single commercial priority?**  
   Choose one: research, waitlist growth, template sales, cohort enrollment, cohort delivery, or premium sales.

### Decision rules

- If 10 research calls do not reveal a clear, repeated pain, refine the niche/message before building more.
- If you get 100+ landing-page visitors but fewer than 10 signups, improve the promise or lead magnet.
- If 12 qualified people express interest in the cohort but fewer than 4 pay, investigate price, timing, format, and clarity—not just “reach.”
- If you have fewer than 4 paid cohort seats by your stated enrollment deadline, postpone or refund rather than running an underfilled cohort that drains time and confidence.
- If premium delivery exceeds five hours, narrow scope or raise pricing before accepting another client.

## 9. The first three experiments to run

## Experiment 1: Niche and problem test

**Hypothesis:** Independent B2B designers in Spain are more motivated by “turn project work into useful content without sounding generic” than by broad “AI content creation” education.

### Run it
- Have 10–12 research calls.
- Publish two LinkedIn posts with different angles:
  1. “AI makes your content generic because you are asking it to invent your expertise.”
  2. “Your best content ideas are probably trapped inside your client projects.”

### Measure
- Replies from independent designers
- Research-call bookings
- Waitlist signups
- Exact language they use

### Pass condition
At least five qualified designers independently identify a version of the same problem and at least 15 join the waitlist or request more information.

---

## Experiment 2: Template product pre-sale

**Hypothesis:** People will pay €29 for a small, practical, bilingual system that helps them use their own work as AI input.

### Run it
- Build a concise beta version.
- Offer 10 founding-user spots.
- Sell before adding extra features.
- Ask each buyer to use one part within seven days.

### Measure
- Number of paid purchases
- Activation: did they complete the Source Bank or first content brief?
- Usability feedback
- Requests for more direct support

### Pass condition
Five paid buyers and at least three who actively use the product.

If it does not sell, do not build a larger template library. Improve the outcome, examples, onboarding, or audience fit.

---

## Experiment 3: Cohort deposit test

**Hypothesis:** The audience will pay for live accountability, feedback, and implementation—not only templates.

### Run it
- Offer the three-week beta sprint at €295.
- Ask for full payment or a meaningful deposit to reserve a seat.
- Hold six seats, with a stated minimum of four.

### Measure
- Qualified applications
- Deposits/paid seats
- Sales-call conversion
- The objections people raise

### Pass condition
Four paid seats from a mix of your existing audience, research participants, and template buyers.

If people are interested but do not pay, ask directly:

> What would need to be different for this to feel like a clear yes or no?

You are looking for a precise answer: date, format, price, outcome, trust, proof, or lack of urgency.

## 10. Responsible-AI principles to make visible

Your ethics are not merely a disclaimer; they are part of the product.

Include these principles on your site and in your teaching:

1. **Start with human expertise and owned source material.**
2. **Do not put confidential client information into public AI tools without permission.**
3. **Fact-check claims, examples, numbers, and citations.**
4. **Do not use AI to fabricate experience, testimonials, case studies, or authority.**
5. **Do not imitate living creators or clients without consent.**
6. **Use AI for assistance: structuring, ideation, editing, analysis, and repurposing—not as a substitute for professional judgment.**
7. **Disclose AI involvement when it is materially relevant to the context or client relationship.**
8. **Respect EU privacy and data-protection responsibilities.**

This creates differentiation. You are not teaching people how to generate more noise. You are helping capable freelancers make their real expertise easier to see, trust, and buy.
