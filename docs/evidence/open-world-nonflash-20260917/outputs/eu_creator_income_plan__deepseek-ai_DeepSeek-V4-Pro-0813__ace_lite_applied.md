# The 90-Day Plan: From €0 to a Realistic Path Toward €3,000/Month

## 1. Positioning

**The core problem your audience has:** They are good at their craft but inconsistent at marketing it. They don't need more generic "post every day" advice. They need a system that fits their actual working life — one that uses AI to reduce the friction of showing up, not to replace their voice.

**Your positioning statement:**

> I help independent designers, copywriters, and consultants in the EU turn their existing expertise into a consistent, non-generic content and offer system — using AI as an assistant, not a replacement. The goal is not to become a content creator. The goal is to make marketing a small, repeatable part of the week that actually brings in clients.

**What you are not:** A growth hacker, a "10x your LinkedIn" guru, or an AI hype channel. You are the practical, bilingual operator who shows freelancers how to do this in 2–3 focused hours per week.

**Why this positioning works for your audience:**
- It respects their craft. You never imply AI replaces their expertise.
- It addresses their real pain: inconsistency, not lack of skill.
- It is specific to the EU freelance market, where English-language advice often misses local client dynamics, pricing norms, and language considerations.

**Positioning in one line (for your LinkedIn headline and email signature):**

> Helping EU freelancers turn expertise into consistent content and client offers — with AI as an assistant, not a replacement.

You have three possible sub-niches within your audience: designers, copywriters, and consultants. For the next 90 days, focus on **one**.

**Recommendation: Independent copywriters and content consultants in Spain and Portugal who serve B2B clients.**

**Why this niche:**

- They are also the most likely to be skeptical of AI — which is perfect for your positioning. You can address their skepticism directly and honestly.

**Niche statement for your content:**

> I help independent B2B copywriters and content consultants in Spain and Portugal build a consistent, non-generic marketing habit — using AI to reduce the friction, not the craft.

- You will not try to serve everyone. Designers and other freelancers can still follow you, but your offers and examples will be built for copywriters and content consultants first.

## 5. The Repeatable Content Series

**Name:** *The Consistent Creator* (or *El Creador Constante* in Spanish)

**Core concept:** Every week, you show one specific way to turn existing client work or expertise into a useful piece of content — using AI to reduce the friction, not the craft.

**The series has three parts, published across three platforms:**

### Part 1 — LinkedIn (Monday or Tuesday)

**Format:** A text post (or short carousel) that teaches one specific, actionable technique.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook:** A specific, relatable problem (e.g., "You finished a client project last week. Now it's gone. No content, no case study, no proof.")
2. **The shift:** One specific way to think about the problem differently
3. **The method:** A step-by-step process (3–5 steps) that includes a specific AI prompt or technique
4. **The result:** What the reader can expect if they do this
5. **Call to action:** A simple, non-pushy invitation (e.g., "Save this for your next client wrap-up" or "Reply with the word 'system' and I'll send you the checklist")

**Example topics:**
- How to turn a client project into 3 LinkedIn posts without sounding like a case study robot
- The exact AI prompt to extract your voice from your best writing
- How to write a newsletter issue in 45 minutes using your own client conversations
- The "voice guard" checklist to keep AI from making you sound generic
- How to turn a client question into a short video script

### Part 2 — Email Newsletter (Wednesday or Thursday)

**Format:** A short, personal email that expands on the LinkedIn post with more depth, a personal story, or a behind-the-scenes look.

**Structure:**
1. **Subject line:** Specific and curiosity-driven (e.g., "The client project you forgot to turn into content")
2. **Opening:** A short personal note or story
3. **The lesson:** The same core idea from the LinkedIn post, but with more depth or a personal example
4. **The tool:** A specific AI prompt, template, or checklist the reader can use
5. **The offer:** A soft mention of the template product or upcoming cohort (not every email — every 2–3 emails)
6. **Signature:** A simple sign-off with your name and a link to your LinkedIn or YouTube

### Part 3 — YouTube (Friday or Saturday)

**Format:** A 5–8 minute video that demonstrates the technique in action.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook:** The same problem from the LinkedIn post, stated in one sentence
2. **Demo:** Screen share or walkthrough of the actual process (e.g., showing the AI prompt in action, building a content plan, editing a draft)
3. **Result:** The finished piece of content or system
4. **Call to action:** Subscribe and comment with a specific question or prompt

**Why this series works:**
- It is repeatable. Every week, you take one client scenario or content challenge and show the process.
- It is specific. You are not giving generic advice. You are showing exact prompts, exact steps, exact results.
- It is honest. You always show the AI as a tool, not a replacement. You show the editing, the judgment, the craft.
- It feeds the funnel. LinkedIn builds awareness, email builds relationship, YouTube builds trust through demonstration.

**Bilingual approach:** Publish the LinkedIn posts in both English and Spanish (either as separate posts or as a bilingual post with a clear structure). The email can be in English with a Spanish summary, or you can segment your list by language preference. The YouTube videos can be in English with Spanish subtitles, or you can alternate languages. For the first 90 days, prioritize English for reach and Spanish for depth with your local audience. Test what resonates.

## 7. Weekly Review Rhythm

**Time:** 1 hour per week, every Friday or Sunday.

**The review has four parts:**

### Part 1 — Numbers (15 minutes)

Review the metrics from the week:
- How many LinkedIn posts, emails, and videos did you publish?
- How many new email subscribers did you get?
- How many meaningful conversations did you have?
- How many template sales or cohort conversations?

### Part 2 — What Worked (15 minutes)

Ask:
- Which post, email, or video got the most engagement or replies?
- Which topic resonated most with your niche?
- Which call to action got the most response?
- What did people ask about or push back on?

### Part 3 — What Didn't Work (15 minutes)

Ask:
- Which post or email fell flat? Why?
- Where did you lose time or energy without results?
- What felt forced or generic?
- What should you stop doing?

### Part 4 — Next Week's Plan (15 minutes)

Decide:
- What is the one content topic for next week?
- What is the one metric you want to improve?
- What is the one experiment you will run?
- What is the one conversation you will start?

**The rule:** No major strategy changes during the week. Make adjustments in the weekly review, not in the moment. This prevents reactive pivoting and keeps you consistent.

## 8. The First Three Experiments

**These are the three experiments to run in the first 30 days. Each is designed to test a specific assumption about your audience and offer.**

### Experiment 1 — The "Client Project to Content" Post (Weeks 1–2)

**Assumption:** Your audience struggles to turn finished client work into content, and they will respond to a specific, step-by-step method for doing this.

**What to do:**
- Write one LinkedIn post that shows the exact process of turning a client project into 3 LinkedIn posts, including the specific AI prompt you would use.
- Make it specific: use a real (anonymized) client scenario from your own work or a hypothetical that feels real.
- End with a simple call to action: "Reply with the word 'system' and I'll send you the checklist."

**What to measure:**
- Number of replies and DMs requesting the checklist
- Number of saves and shares
- Quality of the conversations that result

**What success looks like:** 10+ replies or DMs requesting the checklist, and at least 3 meaningful conversations with people in your niche.

**What you learn:** Whether the "client project to content" angle resonates, and whether your audience is willing to take a small action (replying with a word) to get a resource.

### Experiment 2 — The Free "Consistent Creator Checklist" Lead Magnet (Weeks 2–4)

**Assumption:** A simple, specific lead magnet will convert LinkedIn followers into email subscribers at a meaningful rate.

**What to do:**
- Create a one-page checklist: "The Consistent Creator Checklist: 7 Steps to Turn Your Expertise into Weekly Content Without Sounding Generic" (bilingual).
- Promote it in every LinkedIn post and email for 2 weeks.
- Use a simple landing page (Notion or Carrd) with a clear headline and a single email capture field.

**What to measure:**
- Conversion rate from LinkedIn post views to email signups
- Total new subscribers from the lead magnet
- Open rate and click rate of the welcome email

**What success looks like:** 20–30 new subscribers in 2 weeks, and a welcome email open rate above 50%.

**What you learn:** Whether your audience is willing to exchange their email for a specific, practical resource, and whether your positioning resonates enough to drive signups.

### Experiment 3 — The "Voice Guard" AI Prompt (Weeks 3–4)

**Assumption:** Your audience is skeptical of AI because they fear it will make them sound generic, and they will respond to a tool that specifically addresses this fear.

**What to do:**
- Create a specific AI prompt that helps a freelancer extract their voice from their best writing and apply it to new content.
- Share the prompt in a LinkedIn post and email, with a clear example of the before and after.
- Ask for feedback: "Does this help? What would make it more useful?"

**What to measure:**
- Engagement on the post (comments, saves, shares)
- Number of people who try the prompt and report back
- Quality of the feedback and questions

**What success looks like:** 15+ comments or replies, and at least 5 people who report back that they tried the prompt and found it useful.

**What you learn:** Whether the "voice guard" angle is a strong differentiator for your positioning, and whether your audience is willing to engage with AI tools when the value is specific and honest.

## 10. Honest Risks and How to Handle Them

**Risk 1: The cohort doesn't fill.**
- **Mitigation:** Start with a smaller cohort (5–6 participants). A small, intimate cohort is a feature, not a bug. You can also extend the enrollment period by one week if needed.

**Risk 2: The template product doesn't sell.**
- **Mitigation:** The template is not the main revenue driver. If it doesn't sell, use it as a bonus for cohort participants and a lead magnet for the premium offer. The real value is in the cohort and premium tiers.

**Risk 3: You run out of time.**
- **Mitigation:** The 10-hour weekly budget is realistic, but life happens. If you fall behind, cut the YouTube video first (it is the most time-intensive) and prioritize LinkedIn and email. The cohort is the revenue driver, so protect the time needed to launch it.

**Risk 4: The content feels generic despite your best efforts.**
- **Mitigation:** Every post must include a specific example, a specific prompt, or a specific result. If you can't make it specific, don't publish it. The "voice guard" is not just for your audience — use it on your own content.

**Risk 5: You attract the wrong audience.**
- **Mitigation:** Every week, review your new subscribers and conversations. Are they copywriters and content consultants in your target market? If not, adjust your content to be more specific to their problems. It is better to have 100 relevant subscribers than 1,000 irrelevant ones.
