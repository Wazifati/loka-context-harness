## 1. The Investment Narrative

**The one-sentence story:**
"Northline gives the UK's 5.5 million micro-business owners a simple, forward-looking cash-flow forecast by connecting the three things they already use — invoices, bank feeds, and accounting software — so they can see a cash gap before it hits them, not after."

**The problem (make it visceral):**
Micro-business owners (1–9 employees, under £2m turnover) run their businesses from their bank balance, not their P&L. They use Xero/QuickBooks/FreeAgent for compliance, a bank app for today's balance, and invoicing tools to get paid. None of these tell them: *"In 23 days, you will be £4,200 short because three invoices are late and your VAT bill lands on the 7th."* They discover cash gaps by failed payments, overdraft fees, or not paying themselves.

**Why now:**
- Open Banking and bank feeds are now reliable and cheap in the UK.
- Accounting software APIs (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent) are mature.
- Micro-business owners are increasingly digital but still underserved by enterprise cash-flow tools (Float, Futrli, Fluidly) which are built for accountants or SMEs with finance teams.
- The cost of building this has dropped; the distribution challenge remains.

**The wedge:**
Northline is not another accounting dashboard. It is a **cash-flow early-warning system** for the owner, not the accountant. It answers one question every Monday morning: *"Will I have enough cash in 30, 60, 90 days?"*

**The business model:**
B2B SaaS subscription. Current pilots at £350/month (likely too high for micro-businesses long-term; expect £20–£50/month at scale, but the pilot price proves willingness to pay for a premium, hands-on service). No lending, no credit decisions, no holding funds — this is a software tool only. This keeps the regulatory footprint light and the product focused.

**The ask:**
£750,000 pre-seed for 18 months to:
1. Build a secure, production-grade MVP (bank-grade data handling, Open Banking connections, accounting integrations).
2. Convert 6 design partners into paying customers and run a structured beta with 20–30 micro-businesses.
3. Prove a repeatable go-to-market motion (likely via accountants, bookkeepers, or business banking communities).
4. Reach a credible seed metric: 50–100 paying customers, <5% monthly churn, and a clear customer acquisition cost.

**The honest status:**
"We are pre-product-market fit. We have two paying pilots, six design partners, 41 interviews, and a clickable prototype. We are raising to build the real product and prove retention. We are not claiming traction we don't have."

You are too early for most institutional VCs. You need **pre-seed angels, angel syndicates, and micro-VCs** who:

- Write cheques of £25k–£150k.
- Invest in UK/EU B2B fintech or SaaS at the earliest stage.

- Are comfortable with a 12–18 month path to seed.

3. **Accountancy-tech investors** — people who know the Xero/QuickBooks ecosystem and the power of accountant referrals.
4. **Micro-VCs with a fintech or SMB thesis** — e.g. (do not name specific funds unless you have verified their activity) funds that explicitly say "pre-seed, UK, B2B fintech, £500k–£1m first cheque."

**Avoid:**
- Large VCs who need £1m+ revenue or 10x traction.
- Fintech investors who only back lending, payments, or regulated models.
- Anyone who asks for "proven product-market fit" at pre-seed — they are not your investor.

## 6. Likely Hard Objections and Honest Answers

**Objection 1: "You have no retention data. How do I know people will stick?"**

*Honest answer:* "You don't. That's the single biggest risk in this business, and I won't pretend otherwise. What we do have is two paying pilots who have been using the prototype for [X weeks] and have given us specific feedback on what they'd need to keep paying. We also have 41 interviews where owners told us they check their bank balance daily and would pay for a forward view. But retention is unproven. That's exactly why we're raising: to build the production product and run a structured beta that measures retention properly. If we can't get to <5% monthly churn in 12 months, we shouldn't be funded further."

**Objection 2: "Why not just build this as a feature inside Xero or QuickBooks?"**

*Honest answer:* "They could. But they haven't, and there are good reasons. Xero and QuickBooks are built for accountants and compliance. Their roadmaps are driven by accountant needs, not owner cash-flow anxiety. A cash-flow early-warning system requires a different user experience, different alerts, and a different go-to-market. We're focused on the owner, not the accountant. If a large player decides to build this, that's a risk — but it's also validation of the problem. Our defence is speed, focus, and deep customer intimacy."

**Objection 3: "The pilot price is £350/month. That's not a real SaaS price. How do you know people will pay £30/month?"**

*Honest answer:* "The £350 is for a hands-on pilot where we manually do some of the work. It proves willingness to pay for the *outcome*, not the software. We've tested pricing in interviews: most owners said £20–£30/month is a no-brainer, £50 is a stretch. We'll validate this properly in the beta. If we can't get 50 people to pay £30/month, we don't have a business."

**Objection 4: "This is a feature, not a company."**

*Honest answer:* "It starts as a feature — a cash-flow forecast. But the wedge is the alert. Once an owner trusts Northline to warn them about cash gaps, we can expand to: 'Here's what to do about it' — invoice chasing, payment scheduling, tax reminders, even connecting to affordable credit options (without us making credit decisions). The data we collect on cash-flow patterns across thousands of micro-businesses is itself valuable. But we're not pitching that today. Today, we're pitching the wedge."

**Objection 5: "You have no regulatory authorisation. Isn't this a regulated activity?"**

*Honest answer:* "No. We do not hold customer money, we do not lend, we do not make credit decisions, and we do not give investment advice. We are a software tool that aggregates data the customer already has access to via Open Banking and accounting APIs. We will need to be registered with the FCA as an Account Information Service Provider (AISP) if we access bank data via Open Banking — that's a registration, not a full authorisation, and it's a well-trodden path. We've budgeted for legal and compliance support in the raise. If we ever move into payments or credit, that's a different conversation and a different regulatory regime."

**Objection 6: "The market is crowded. Float, Futrli, Fluidly, and others already do cash-flow forecasting."**

*Honest answer:* "They do, and they're good products. But they are built for accountants and SMEs with finance teams. They are not built for the 5.5 million micro-businesses with 1–9 employees who don't have a finance person. Our interviews show these owners find existing tools too complex, too accountant-focused, and too expensive. We're building for the owner who checks their bank balance on their phone, not the bookkeeper who reconciles transactions. That's a different user, a different product, and a different distribution channel."

**Objection 7: "Why should I back you instead of a team with more fintech experience?"**

*Honest answer:* "Because we've spent more time with the actual customer than most fintech teams. 41 interviews, 6 design partners, 2 paying pilots — that's not a huge number, but it's enough to know the problem deeply. Our technical cofounder has [specific relevant experience]. Our commercial founder has [specific relevant experience]. We're not claiming to be the most experienced team in fintech. We're claiming to be the most focused on this specific problem. And we're raising a modest amount to prove it."

**Objection 8: "What's your valuation?"**

*Honest answer:* "We're open to discussion, but we're thinking in the range of £3–4 million pre-money for a £750k raise. That's in line with UK pre-seed fintech deals where there's a prototype, early revenue, and a clear problem. We're not going to negotiate against ourselves, but we're also not going to be unrealistic. The most important thing is finding the right partner."

## Final Advice

1. **Do not overclaim.** Your honesty about being pre-product-market fit is a strength. Investors at this stage are backing your ability to learn and execute, not your current metrics.
2. **Use the 30 days to create urgency.** Two paying pilots, fresh interviews, and a sharpened deck will make you more investable than you are today.
3. **Target the right investors.** A £750k pre-seed round is too small for most VCs. Focus on angels, syndicates, and micro-VCs who understand the stage.
4. **Prepare for the "no" and ask for referrals.** Every investor conversation is a chance to learn and expand your network.
5. **Keep the product focused.** Do not add lending, credit, or investment features. That changes your regulatory status and dilutes your story.

You have enough to start. Now go and make the next 30 days count.
