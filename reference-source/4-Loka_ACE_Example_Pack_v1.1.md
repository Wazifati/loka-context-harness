## What This File Is

This Example Pack shows how to use **Loka_ACE — Adaptive Context Engine** across common business, research, content, planning, and operations use cases.

Loka_ACE turns a vague request into a structured AI operating context using the ACE-6 model:

1. **Aim** — the true objective
2. **Character** — the useful role or operating identity
3. **Execution** — the process the AI must follow
4. **Constraints** — rules, boundaries, and forbidden behavior
5. **Export** — the final output format
6. **Evaluation** — how success will be judged

Use these examples as patterns. Replace the bracketed text with your own context.

---

## How To Use These Examples

1. Choose the example closest to your task.
2. Copy the prompt.
3. Replace the bracketed fields.
4. Paste it into your AI tool.
5. Review the output against the evaluation criteria.

For simple tasks, use the example as-is.  
For sensitive, technical, legal, financial, health, security, or production-bound work, add expert review and use the appropriate specialized framework or professional process.

# Example 1 — Market Research Report

## Use Case

You want to research whether a product, service, market, or audience segment is worth pursuing.

## Copy-Ready Prompt

```text
Use Loka_ACE.

A1 — Aim
Determine whether there is meaningful market demand for [product/service/category] among [target audience]. The goal is not to produce a promotional report. The goal is to help me make a better business decision.

A2 — Character
Act as a skeptical market researcher and business analyst. You understand customer segmentation, competitor mapping, pricing, adoption barriers, and go-to-market strategy. Avoid hype and separate facts from assumptions.

A3 — Execution
Follow this process:
1. Define the market problem and why it matters.
2. Identify the strongest target customer segments.
3. Map direct competitors, indirect competitors, and substitutes.
4. Identify demand signals from credible sources, public discussions, product launches, funding, search behavior, or customer pain points.
5. Analyze monetization options and willingness to pay.
6. Identify risks, objections, and reasons the idea could fail.
7. Recommend the most practical validation steps.
8. Give a final verdict with confidence level.

A4 — Constraints
- Separate facts, assumptions, and inferences.
- Do not assume demand equals willingness to pay.
- Do not overstate the opportunity.
- Include substitutes, not only direct competitors.
- Highlight weak evidence and missing information.
- Use clear, practical language.

A5 — Export
Produce a structured report with these sections:
1. Executive Summary
2. Market Problem
3. Target Customer Segments
4. Competitor and Substitute Map
5. Demand Signals
6. Monetization Options
7. Risks and Objections
8. Validation Plan
9. Final Verdict
10. Source List

A6 — Evaluation
The report will be judged on:
- Source quality
- Clarity of customer segments
- Strength of competitor mapping
- Honesty about uncertainty
- Practicality of validation steps
- Usefulness for making a go/no-go decision
```

# Example 2 — Go-To-Market Strategy

## Use Case

You have a product or service idea and need a practical launch plan.

## Copy-Ready Prompt

```text
Use Loka_ACE.

A1 — Aim
Create a practical go-to-market strategy for [product/service] aimed at [target audience]. The goal is to identify the fastest credible path to early traction, not to create a generic marketing plan.

A2 — Character
Act as a pragmatic go-to-market strategist for early-stage businesses. Think in terms of positioning, customer pain, distribution channels, offer design, trust-building, and measurable experiments.

A3 — Execution
Follow this process:
1. Clarify the customer, pain point, and desired outcome.
2. Identify the strongest positioning angle.
3. Define the first offer and why someone would buy it now.
4. Recommend the most realistic acquisition channels.
5. Design a 30-day launch plan.
6. Design a 90-day validation plan.
7. Identify the main objections and how to address them.
8. Define success metrics and kill criteria.

A4 — Constraints
- Do not suggest channels that require a large audience unless stated.
- Do not assume paid ads are the answer.
- Prioritize low-cost, high-learning actions.
- Separate brand-building from direct acquisition.
- Be specific, not motivational.

A5 — Export
Produce:
1. One-line positioning
2. Ideal customer profile
3. First offer
4. Channel strategy
5. 30-day plan
6. 90-day plan
7. Messaging angles
8. Objections and responses
9. Metrics to track
10. What would invalidate the strategy

A6 — Evaluation
The strategy will be judged on:
- Specificity
- Cost realism
- Speed to learning
- Fit with target customer behavior
- Clarity of the first offer
- Whether the plan can be executed by a small team
```

1. Product summary
2. Target users
3. Problem statement
4. Core value proposition
5. MVP features
6. Later features
7. Out of scope
8. Core user flows

10. Open questions

- Clarity
- MVP discipline
- User focus
- Reduced ambiguity
- Usefulness for handoff
- Whether it avoids unnecessary complexity

# Final Note

The strongest Loka_ACE prompts do not rely on persona alone. The load-bearing parts are usually:

- **Aim** — a clear objective
- **Execution** — a reliable process
- **Constraints** — clear rules and boundaries
- **Evaluation** — visible success criteria

Use Character to focus the style of thinking, but use Execution, Constraints, and Evaluation to control quality.
